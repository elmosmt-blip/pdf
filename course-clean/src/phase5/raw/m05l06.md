## Why fine-pitch QFP placement is a data problem
A fine-pitch QFP exposes placement error because the positional margin is small next to the lead pitch. On a 0.5 mm pitch part, a lateral shift of 0.10 mm already eats 20 % of the pitch, and 0.5 degrees of rotation moves an outer lead by tens of micrometres depending on body size.

The practical aim is to separate true placement error from measurement noise, paste print variation and reflow-induced movement, using placement verification, AOI, SPI and reflow inspection in sequence rather than one end-of-line image.

## Offset, skew and rotation
Offset is translation of the component centre from the intended centre, reported as X and Y error. Skew is a positional mismatch between opposite sides, seen as one side closer to its pads than the other. Rotation is angular error about the centre.

On a QFP a small rotation looks like skew when measured at one corner, so measure two opposite corners or use fiducial-based centre and angle data. For fine-pitch QFPs, placement targets usually sit around ±0.05 mm to ±0.10 mm for centre position and ±0.5° to ±1.0° for angle, verified against the placement machine datasheet, the component datasheet and the IPC-A-610 class requirements.

## Missing, wrong-part and polarity are a different family
A missing QFP is usually a feeder, vision or placement-cycle failure rather than a subtle drift. Wrong-part defects come from feeder misload, wrong program selection or gaps in barcode and serialisation control.

Polarity defects on QFPs show at the pin 1 marking, chamfer, dot or bevel, and the program can miss them with the wrong part orientation or a backwards tape. These defects are binary — present or absent, correct or reversed — and belong apart from the dimensional errors of offset, skew and rotation.

## Attributing defects with the evidence sequence
The strongest sequence is SPI before placement, placement verification during placement, AOI after placement, and final inspection after reflow.

Acceptable paste with a component placed outside tolerance makes placement the likely cause. Paste offset, bridging or volume variation that the component follows makes printing the likely cause. An acceptable part after placement that moves during reflow makes reflow the cause, through wetting imbalance, paste collapse or thermal effects. Do not blame placement when the pre-placement evidence already shows the same pattern.

## Common placement mechanisms on fine-pitch QFP
Poor fiducial recognition, camera calibration drift, nozzle wear, vacuum loss, feeder repeatability, tape peel variation and program coordinate errors all appear on this package.

Nozzle choice matters: a nozzle that presses the body too hard tilts the part, and poor vacuum lets it shift in transfer. Feeder problems often produce repeatable skew or rotation on one board side, because the part is presented at a slightly inconsistent angle. Program errors give a consistent offset across many boards, while mechanical wear gives gradual drift or intermittent outliers.

## Separating placement from printing and reflow
Printing defects show as paste offset, volume variation or shape distortion at pad level. A component that follows the paste points to printing.

Placement defects show as position or angle error while the paste stays acceptable. Reflow defects show as movement after the paste melted: acceptable post-placement AOI but shifted post-reflow AOI, tombstoning or coplanarity problems. Surface tension can correct small errors on a fine-pitch QFP only where paste volume and pad geometry are balanced, so partial self-centring points at reflow.

## A practical diagnosis workflow
Confirm the defect type first: missing, wrong part, polarity, offset, skew or rotation. Then establish whether it is random, dependent on board position, or repeatable across boards.

Random missing parts point to feeder or vacuum. Repeatable offset or rotation points to program, fiducial or calibration. Board-position-dependent skew points to table flatness, support or nozzle transfer. A defect that appears only after reflow needs pre- and post-reflow measurements compared, and a defect that appears at SPI needs printing investigated before placement.

## Standards and internal limits together
IPC-A-610 gives the acceptability criteria for placement and soldering without replacing machine capability targets or design-specific limits. IPC-7351 supplies land pattern and package data for pad-to-lead relationships, and IPC-7525 and the related stencil guidance help evaluate paste print capability, with exact aperture and volume targets verified against your paste, stencil and printer datasheets.

Keep internal limits tighter than the final acceptability limit, so placement can be corrected before a solder defect appears.

## Key takeaways
Measure centre offset and rotation on two opposite corners, because a small rotation at one corner reads as skew.
Attribute with the evidence sequence — SPI, placement, AOI, reflow — and do not blame placement when the paste already shows the pattern.
Set internal placement limits tighter than IPC-A-610 acceptance, so correction happens before a solder defect exists.

## Common mistakes
Judging a QFP from a single end-of-line image :: Compare pre-placement, post-placement and post-reflow data before naming the mechanism.
Treating a repeatable offset as reflow self-centring :: Check whether the offset exists at post-placement AOI, which settles the question.
Blaming the program for drift that repeats by board location :: Check table flatness, support and feeder presentation, which produce location-dependent patterns.

## Glossary
Centre offset :: The translation of a component centre from its intended position, reported as X and Y.
Rotation error :: Angular displacement of the component about its centre after placement.
Wetting imbalance :: Uneven solder wetting that pulls a component out of position during reflow.
Coplanarity :: How well the leads or terminations of a component lie in one plane.
