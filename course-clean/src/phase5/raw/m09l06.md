## Start with the solder-side image
A wave defect atlas starts on the solder side, because the defect shape records the last thermal and wetting events.

Read fill height, meniscus, wetting angle, solder volume, and whether the defect is isolated or repeats with component mass. Compare images in the same field of view. Acceptance limits live in IPC-A-610, so confirm the applicable class and revision before judging a joint.

## Bridging
Bridging is solder between adjacent pads or leads after the wave. Too much solder volume, poor drainage, low preheat, a slow conveyor or flux residue all cause it.

On fine pitch, check wave height, solder temperature and flux coverage. Wave height windows depend on the equipment, so verify against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Balling
Balling is separate solder balls on pads, traces or component bodies, and it usually means poor wetting or premature solidification: low preheat, oxidised leads, weak flux activity, or a solder temperature too low for the alloy.

Excessive turbulence and poor drainage can also throw balls. Compare the ball location with the wave contact pattern, and check preheat and solder temperature against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Insufficient fill
Insufficient fill shows as a low solder column in a through-hole, often with a shiny meniscus at the bottom and little wetting on the lead.

The responsible parameter is usually preheat ramp or dwell: the top of the hole never reaches the temperature capillary action needs. High aspect ratio, board thickness and lead-to-hole clearance all contribute. Typical preheat ramps are 1.0 to 3.0 °C/s and dwell 2 to 5 s, with 3 to 5 s for lead-free, verified against the flux and equipment datasheet.

## Bypass
Bypass is a joint the wave never wetted, showing a dry pad or lead with little solder.

Wave height too low, board warpage, poor flux coverage, a conveyor too fast, or a shielding effect from nearby components all produce it. Check the wave contact line and board support, then verify wave height, conveyor speed and spray pattern against the equipment datasheet.

## Lifted lead
A lifted lead has separated from the pad or barrel after wave contact, sometimes leaving a small solder remnant.

It is usually thermal-mechanical: high component mass, rapid heating, board expansion or insufficient preheat create differential movement. Handling and mounting contribute too. Check preheat ramp, component mass and board support against the process specification.

## Blowholes
Blowholes appear as voids or craters in the joint, often near the hole wall or under the component. Trapped moisture, solvent or flux volatiles boiling during wave contact cause them.

A fast preheat ramp, low top-side temperature, high aspect ratio or a contaminated board all raise the risk. Check preheat ramp, dwell and board bake requirements against your paste and equipment datasheet and the IPC-A-610 acceptance criteria.

## From image to parameter
Classify the image by shape first: bridging, balling, low fill, dry joint, lifted lead or void.

Then map the shape to a parameter — volume and drainage for bridging, wetting temperature for balling, preheat and dwell for insufficient fill, wave contact for bypass, thermal mass and support for a lifted lead, moisture and volatiles for blowholes.

Confirm with a short DOE that changes one parameter at a time, and record the image, the profile and the datasheet limits behind the change.

## Key takeaways
Read the solder side first, because fill height and meniscus record what the wave actually did.
Map each shape to one parameter before touching the machine, since the appearance already narrows the candidates.
Confirm with a single-parameter DOE and keep the image with the profile, so the fix is reproducible.

## Common mistakes
Raising solder temperature to cure balling :: Check preheat, lead oxidation and flux activity first, since cold preheat is the usual cause.
Treating a lifted lead as a solder defect :: Review preheat ramp, component mass and board support, which is where the movement comes from.
Reading fill height without the aspect ratio :: Note hole depth and diameter, because a deep narrow hole needs more energy to fill.

## Glossary
Bypass :: A joint that the wave failed to wet at all.
Blowhole :: A void or crater in the joint caused by expanding trapped volatiles.
Aspect ratio (plated through-hole) :: Hole depth divided by hole diameter, the same name as the stencil ratio but a different quantity.
Meniscus :: The curved solder surface at the top of a filled hole or at the joint edge.
