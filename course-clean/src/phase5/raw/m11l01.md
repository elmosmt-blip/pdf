## Station anatomy and the control loop
A hand soldering station is a closed loop: controller, heater, tip, sensor and ground path.

The setpoint is the tip temperature at the heater, not the joint temperature. A good station recovers quickly when a cold joint draws heat, so choose a station whose tip holds a stable temperature while it touches the pad, and confirm the actual tip temperature with a calibrated thermocouple or probe against the equipment datasheet.

IPC J-STD-001 expects workmanship that does not damage the base material, which makes a controlled, clean and grounded station part of the requirement rather than a convenience.

## Tip selection
The tip is the heat-transfer interface, so geometry follows the joint.

Small conical or chisel tips suit chip components, wider chisel or hoof tips suit through-hole leads and ground planes, and specialty shapes cover fine-pitch or BGA rework. Low thermal mass recovers faster on small joints; higher thermal mass holds temperature on large pads.

The contact face must be clean, tinned and free of oxidation. Check tip compatibility and power rating against the iron datasheet, because an undersized tip drives long dwell while an oversized one heats neighbouring parts.

## Temperature setting and alloy
Setpoint selection starts with the alloy, the flux and the thermal mass.

Lead-free SAC alloys generally need higher process temperatures than eutectic tin-lead, and the exact range depends on paste, iron and board design, so verify against your paste and equipment datasheet.

A higher setpoint shortens contact time and can also oxidise flux, damage laminate or lift pads. A lower one produces cold joints when the tip cannot recover. Use the lowest temperature that wets and forms a fillet inside an acceptable cycle time.

## Technique and thermal budget
Good technique is repeatable contact, not scraping.

Clean the tip, add a little fresh solder, touch the tip to pad and terminal, feed solder into the joint, remove the tip, and hold the part until the solder solidifies.

The thermal budget combines tip temperature, contact time, heat-transfer area and the number of heat cycles. Record setpoint, tip type, contact time and any preheat, and validate the budget on scrap or test coupons, because IPC J-STD-001 requires a sound wetted joint without heat damage.

## ESD control at the bench
Hand soldering means handling bare boards and components, so the station belongs to an ESD control program.

ANSI/ESD S20.20 and IEC 61340-5-1 cover grounded equipment, personnel grounding, conductive or dissipative work surfaces, and verification. Bond the iron tip and body to a common ground point, and ground the operator where the program requires it.

Verify the iron ground path and the wrist strap or footwear system with the tester your ESD program specifies, and replace damaged cords, mats and straps.

## Fume control
Solder fume carries flux decomposition products and particulate, so local exhaust is the practical control.

Place the extraction inlet close to the joint without blocking the operator's view, and hold the capture velocity the ventilation design calls for. Check airflow, filter condition and face velocity against the equipment datasheet and local occupational exposure limits.

On a shared hood, keep the inlet near the source and never between the operator's breathing zone and the joint.

## Inspection and process control
After soldering, inspect wetting, fillet shape, solder volume, lifted pads, charred laminate, solder balls and disturbed components.

IPC-A-610 gives the visual acceptance criteria and IPC J-STD-001 covers workmanship and repair practice. Use a magnifier or microscope sized to the component.

When a joint fails, decide whether heat, contamination, insufficient solder or mechanical damage caused it, then correct the process rather than reworking repeatedly without a root cause.

## Key takeaways
Measure the tip temperature with a calibrated probe, because the setpoint is the heater temperature and not the joint temperature.
Pick the tip by joint geometry and thermal mass, since an undersized tip forces long dwell and an oversized one heats the neighbours.
Record the thermal budget with the setpoint, tip and contact time, so a rework can be reproduced and audited.

## Common mistakes
Setting the iron as hot as it goes to shorten contact :: Use the lowest temperature that wets, because excess heat lifts pads and chars laminate.
Reworking a failed joint repeatedly without a cause :: Identify whether heat, contamination or solder volume caused the failure first.
Checking ESD grounding once at installation :: Verify the iron and the operator grounding at the interval the program defines.

## Glossary
Thermal budget :: The combination of tip temperature, contact time and heat cycles a joint receives.
Wetting :: The spreading of molten solder into a continuous metallurgical bond with the pad and lead.
Capture velocity :: The air speed at the extraction inlet needed to pull fume away from the joint.
Hoof tip :: A curved tip shape that carries a solder bead to a joint, useful on through-hole and large pads.
