## Pad stack and stencil thickness trade-off
Foil thickness sets paste volume per aperture. For a given pad stack, pick a thickness that delivers enough paste for fillet and standoff without the excess that bridges or the shortage that opens.

Selection usually starts from the smallest pitch and the smallest aperture in the assembly, then gets checked against the larger components. Common thicknesses are 0.10 mm to 0.15 mm for fine-pitch work and 0.12 mm to 0.18 mm for mixed boards, subject to the stencil supplier's capability and the paste datasheet. IPC-7525 covers thickness, aperture area and sidewall finish.

## Chip components: aperture area and paste balance
For chip resistors and capacitors the aperture should match pad size while still allowing paste to form a wettable fillet. A common starting point is aperture area at 80 % to 100 % of pad area, with less area on the smallest parts to prevent tombstoning and bridging.

On the smallest chips the aperture width may be 0.10 mm to 0.15 mm and the length 0.20 mm to 0.30 mm, but the final values come from IPC-7525, the IPC-7351 land pattern and the observed release. Where the pad sits on a large copper area, reduce aperture area so the extra heat does not turn into excess paste and solder balling.

## QFP fine pitch: area ratio and sidewall control
Gull-wing fine-pitch packages need enough paste to wet the lead foot while keeping solder mask clearance between adjacent pads. Area ratio — aperture opening area divided by aperture wall area — is the useful control, and IPC-7525 discusses how it drives release.

A practical target is 0.66 or higher for small apertures, with sidewall polish and paste viscosity adjusted when the ratio falls below. Below 0.5 mm pitch, narrow the aperture relative to the pad, use trapezoidal or rounded shapes, and check the result against IPC-7525 and the paste datasheet to keep bridges out.

## QFN and LGA: peripheral pads and thermal escape
QFN and LGA packages usually carry peripheral pads plus a central thermal pad. Peripheral apertures should be sized to avoid coplanarity defects and bridging, while the centre aperture is segmented or reduced to control volume and let gas escape.

A common approach divides the thermal pad into several small apertures with 0.10 mm to 0.20 mm between segments, or uses a reduced-area pattern; check it against IPC-7525 and the IPC-7351 land pattern guidance. An oversized centre pad traps paste and causes voiding, non-wetting or package lift, while an undersized one costs thermal performance and joint reliability.

## BGA: pitch, aperture area and collapse
On BGA the geometry follows ball pitch, ball diameter and paste collapse during reflow. IPC-7095 covers BGA design and assembly, including paste deposition and land pattern considerations.

Apertures are usually smaller than the ball diameter to limit paste volume and bridging, especially at 0.5 mm pitch and below. At larger pitches the aperture may approach the land area, but paste rheology and the reflow profile still have to be verified. Where BGA pads share a board with fine-pitch parts, a step stencil or local thickness change can serve both.

## Foil thickness for mixed component sets
Find the smallest pitch and smallest aperture that must release paste reliably, then choose thickness against them. A thinner foil cuts volume and keeps bridges off fine-pitch QFP, QFN and BGA pads, but it can starve larger components and open joints when the paste fails to wet the pad. A thicker foil feeds the large parts and coplanarity, and raises the bridging risk on fine pitch.

On a board with both, consider a step stencil or local thickness change, and check the transition location against IPC-7525 and what the stencil fabricator can hold.

## Aperture geometry and process verification
Aperture shape, wall finish and aperture reduction are process controls, not drawing decoration. Rounded corners, trapezoidal apertures and reduced-area patterns can improve release and cut bridging, but they have to be tested with the actual paste, squeegee pressure and print speed.

Judge joints against IPC-A-610 and confirm the printed deposit with paste volume measurement or two- and three-dimensional inspection. Values such as area-ratio targets, reduction percentages and thickness ranges are practice-based, so check them against your paste and equipment datasheet and your first-article results.

## Mapping geometry to defect prevention
Tie every geometry choice to a defect it prevents. Adequate area ratio with clean sidewalls prevents insufficient paste and opens; reduced aperture area with thinner foil prevents fine-pitch bridging; segmented centre pads prevent voiding and lift on QFN and LGA; controlled BGA aperture area prevents collapse and bridging.

The choice is a balance. The thickness that prevents opens on a large pad can bridge a 0.4 mm pitch QFP, so justify the final value by the dominant defect risk and confirm it by inspection.

## Key takeaways
Start thickness selection at the smallest pitch and smallest aperture, then check that the large components still get their volume.
Segment the QFN thermal pad so gas can escape, because a solid centre aperture traps paste and voids the joint.
Justify each geometry choice by the defect it prevents, and confirm the result with SPI and IPC-A-610 inspection.

## Common mistakes
Using one foil thickness for a board with 0.4 mm QFP and large thermal pads :: Step the stencil or accept a compromise on the dominant defect risk, and verify both areas.
Filling the QFN centre pad with one large aperture :: Segment it into small apertures with 0.10 mm to 0.20 mm gaps so volatiles escape.
Sizing chip apertures at full pad area on the smallest parts :: Reduce area to prevent tombstoning and bridging, then verify with SPI.

## Glossary
Thermal pad :: The large central land under a QFN or LGA that carries heat and needs a controlled paste volume.
Tombstoning :: One end of a chip component lifting during reflow because the two joints wet at different rates.
Aperture reduction :: Deliberately making the stencil opening smaller than the land to control paste volume and bridging.
Land pattern :: The copper geometry on the board that the aperture and the component termination must match.
