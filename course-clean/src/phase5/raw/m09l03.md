## The thermal path in wave soldering
Heat enters a wave-soldered board mainly from the bottom, so the bottom surface rises quickly while the top lags.

The top surface temperature is the practical control point for flux activation and solvent evaporation. Put thermocouples on the bottom near the wave contact zone and on the top over a representative component or pad. For a typical 1.6 mm FR-4 board, a top-surface target of 90-130 °C just before the wave is a common starting point, verified against the flux datasheet, board thickness, copper content and component sensitivity.

## Flux type selection
Select flux by residue requirement, cleaning capability and hole-fill need.

No-clean fluxes suit lines without cleaning and where residue can stay, and they may leave visible residue and demand tighter preheat control. Water-soluble fluxes serve through-hole fill where aqueous cleaning exists, and they can be more corrosive when left on. Rosin-based fluxes remain in some repair and legacy processes. Take the classification and residue requirements from IPC J-STD-004 and confirm compatibility with the finish and the cleaning process.

## Flux amount
Flux has to wet every bottom pad, lead tip and hole wall without boiling violently, lifting components or creating fog.

For spray application the controlled quantity is solids per unit of bottom area: a starting range of 0.08-0.23 mg/cm2, which is 500-1500 µg/in² of solids and about 0.8-2.3 g/m², with the lower end for thin boards and the higher end for heavy copper or through-hole walls. Figures quoted per unit of liquid, such as 0.5-1.0 g/ft², are several times larger and not comparable.

For foam application, set foam height and dwell so flux reaches the hole walls without dripping. Verify the amount by weighing a test board before and after fluxing, against the supplier's solids and activity data.

## Preheat level, top against bottom
Preheat raises the board enough to activate flux and evaporate solvents without thermal shock or premature flux degradation.

A practical target is 90-130 °C on the top surface and 100-140 °C on the bottom immediately before wave contact, on a controlled ramp. Thin boards need less preheat to protect components, while thick boards and heavy copper planes need more to lift the top surface. Profile to IPC-7530 guidance and adjust until hole fill and wetting are stable.

## Wave height and fog control
Wave height must wet bottom pads and lead tips without flooding the top side or stirring up turbulence.

For a 1.6 mm board a starting height of 0.5-1.5 mm above the bottom surface is common, checked visually and by looking for solder balls, bridging and top-side contamination. Spray pressure, nozzle distance, flux viscosity and preheat control the fog: a fine even mist is what you want, and dripping flux or visible fog on nearby surfaces means too much application or poor atomisation.

## Setting a process for a given board
A thin board with light copper and small through-hole leads starts with lower flux density, lower preheat and lower wave height.

A thick board, heavy copper or large leads gets more preheat first, then more flux, then more wave height only if wetting is still short. A cold top side calls for more preheat or a slower conveyor; an overheating bottom calls for less wave height or lower wave temperature. Record the settings that give acceptable fill without solder balls, bridging or component damage.

## Verification and acceptance
Verify with thermocouple data, flux weight gain and joint inspection. Check hole fill, wetting angle, residue, solder balls, bridging and component damage against IPC-A-610 for the required class.

Document flux type, flux density, preheat setpoints, wave height, conveyor speed and wave temperature. After any change, re-run the profile and inspect a representative board before production.

## Key takeaways
Control the top-surface temperature before the wave, because the bottom heats faster and hides a cold top side.
Work in flux solids per unit area, since liquid-volume figures differ by several times and cannot be compared.
Raise preheat before flux and wave height when a thick board fills poorly, because temperature is usually the first limit.

## Common mistakes
Measuring preheat on the bottom side only :: Add a top thermocouple, because flux activation depends on the top surface.
Comparing a g/ft² liquid figure with a solids deposition :: Convert to solids per unit area before comparing the two.
Raising wave height to improve fill on a heavy board :: Increase preheat first, since a higher wave brings bridging and top-side contamination.

## Glossary
Fog height :: The height of the flux mist applied to the board underside.
Solids deposition :: The mass of flux solids left per unit of board area.
Top-surface temperature :: The board temperature on the component side just before wave contact.
Thermal shock :: Rapid temperature change that can crack components or delaminate the board.
