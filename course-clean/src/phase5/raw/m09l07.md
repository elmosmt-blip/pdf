## The process control objective
Wave control sets the thermal and chemical conditions that let solder wet through-hole barrels and solder-side terminations without bridges, non-wetting or spatter.

The controls are flux type and amount, preheat temperature, wave or fog height, conveyor speed and pot temperature. For a given board, justify each one from board mass, copper content, component heat sinks, the flux activation range and the alloy liquidus.

## Flux type selection
Choose the flux from the contamination level and the residue requirement.

Rosin mildly activated flux is common on general through-hole assemblies where residues are cleaned or acceptable. Water-soluble flux suits a high oxide load or a planned cleaning step. No-clean fluxes appear less often at the wave, because residue and spatter are harder to manage there; check compatibility against the flux datasheet and the cleaning process.

## Flux amount
Control flux as a deposition of solids per unit area of the bottom side. A practical starting range is 0.08 to 0.23 mg/cm2 of solids, which is 500-1500 µg/in² and about 0.8-2.3 g/m², verified against the flux datasheet and the spray pattern.

Wet mass per unit area is several times larger because the flux is roughly 2 % solids, so state whether a figure is on a solids or a liquid basis before comparing it. Too little flux gives non-wetting and dull joints; too much gives spatter, residue and poor fill. Weight a test coupon or board before and after fluxing to confirm coverage.

## Preheat level
Preheat activates flux, removes volatiles and reduces thermal shock.

Measure the top surface or a representative thermal mass with a thermocouple. A practical entry range is 90 to 120 °C for many boards, with the flux activation temperature and the board maximum setting the final value. A cold board boils flux violently and spatters; a hot one burns the flux out before the wave arrives.

## Wave height and fog height
Wave height decides how much solder contacts the board bottom.

On a single wave, a crest 1 to 2 mm above the nozzle is a common starting point. On a dual-wave machine the first wave may sit near 1 to 2 mm and the second near 0.5 to 1 mm, with fog height often 2 to 5 mm above the nozzle. Check the equipment manual, because nozzle geometry changes the relationship between crest height and board immersion.

## Contact time and pot temperature
Contact time is wave length divided by conveyor speed. A practical range is 2 to 4 s for many boards, adjusted for hole fill and component heat sinks.

Pot temperature must sit above the alloy liquidus: 245 to 255 °C is common for Sn63/Pb37 and 250 to 260 °C for SAC305. Verify against the alloy datasheet and equipment capability, and record the actual pot temperature.

## Bath contamination and tin consumption
Bath condition changes wetting and the defect rate. Copper, nickel, iron and flux residues reach the bath from boards, fixtures and nozzles, so monitor contamination against the solder supplier's limits and change or filter the bath when wetting degrades.

Tin leaves through dross, drag-out, evaporation and replenishment losses. Track solder added per board or per hour, dross handling and pot level to keep wave height stable.

## Records and first article
Record flux type, lot, wet flux density, preheat temperature, wave height, fog height, conveyor speed, pot temperature and any deviation.

For the first article, run the board, inspect the solder side against IPC-A-610 Class 2 or 3, measure hole fill and keep a solder-side image. If it fails, change one parameter at a time and record the result.

## Key takeaways
Justify each setting from board mass, copper and alloy, since the same recipe moves everything when the board changes.
State flux figures on a solids or liquid basis before comparing them, because the two differ by several times.
Keep the first-article image with the parameter record, so the next run starts from evidence rather than memory.

## Common mistakes
Setting pot temperature below the alloy liquidus :: Confirm the liquidus from the alloy datasheet, since a cold pot cannot wet.
Recording settings without the flux lot :: Include the lot, because bath and flux condition drift with it.
Changing preheat, wave height and speed together on a failing first article :: Change one parameter at a time, or the cause is lost.

## Glossary
Pot temperature :: The temperature of the molten solder in the wave reservoir.
Crest height :: The height of the solder wave above the nozzle opening.
Dross :: Oxide and intermetallic debris that forms on the molten solder surface.
Flux activation range :: The temperature band in which the flux removes oxides effectively.
