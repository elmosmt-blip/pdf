## What the profile controls
A reflow profile is the temperature history the board and its components actually see, not the oven setpoints. Ramp, soak, time above liquidus, peak and cooling describe it, and between them they govern flux activation, flux removal, wetting, intermetallic formation, voiding and thermal stress.

Because thermal mass differs, the same oven recipe produces different results on a different assembly, so measure the profile on the board. The best profile is not the hottest or the fastest: it is the exposure that makes reliable joints while keeping board and components inside their qualified limits.

## Ramp and soak
The ramp carries the assembly to the temperature where flux activates and solvents leave without violent boiling. Typical soak ranges are 140-180 °C for 90-120 s, checked against the paste and equipment datasheet.

A heavy board with large copper planes may need a longer soak to narrow the gap between small components and large connectors. Too short a soak leaves flux active too late, which raises solder balls and voiding; too long dries the paste and reduces wetting. Judge the soak from measured board temperature, never from oven air temperature.

## Time above liquidus
Time above liquidus is how long the alloy stays molten. It has to be long enough for wetting and flux removal and short enough to limit intermetallic growth and component exposure.

J-STD-020 bounds it: its classification profile sets tL, the time at liquidus, at 60-150 s for both alloy families. IPC-7530 and the paste datasheet narrow that into a working window — IPC-7530 typically calls for 30-90 s above liquidus, and the paste datasheet is the authority for the board being run. J-STD-020's own time figure, tp, is the time within 5 °C of the classification temperature, limited to 30 s on the Pb-free profile and 20 s for SnPb; it is a package qualification parameter, not the paste window for running a board.

Production profiles may use a different window if qualified. Take the liquidus temperature from the paste or alloy datasheet, because it depends on alloy and formulation, and check the measured time above liquidus at the worst-case thermocouple rather than at a convenient point.

## Peak temperature
Peak temperature is the highest measured temperature at the solder joint or component body, depending on where the thermocouple sits.

For SAC305, J-STD-020 gives no single peak: it gives classification temperatures by package size — 260 °C for small packages, 250 °C for large and 245 °C for very large at 1.6-2.5 mm thickness — and the full table is thickness times volume, where below 1.6 mm every volume allows 260 °C and above 2.5 mm the limits fall to 250 °C small and 245 °C large and very large. Each comes with tp, the time within 5 °C of that temperature, limited to 30 s.

Production peaks usually sit below the classification temperature with margin and are verified with thermocouples on the actual board. Exceeding the classification temperature damages components, delaminates packages or grows excessive intermetallic, and the peak must be judged together with time above liquidus, because a short peak and a long peak are not the same thermal exposure.

## Cooling
Cooling solidifies the joint and sets grain size, residual stress and warpage. Typical rates are 1-4 °C/s, verified against the paste and equipment datasheet.

Very fast cooling raises thermal shock and warpage, especially on thin boards and large BGAs. Very slow cooling produces a coarse solder structure and lets components move while the joint is still soft. Measure cooling with the same board thermocouples used for peak and time above liquidus, because the cooling curve reveals lag and gradients that the oven profile hides.

## Why thermocouples go on the board
Oven air temperature is not solder joint temperature. Board thickness, copper area, component mass, shielding and mounting method all create lag and gradients, so attach calibrated thermocouples with high-temperature adhesive or tape and minimal added mass.

Representative points are the centre of a large BGA, a small passive near a heavy connector, and a location near the board edge or panel rail. A thermocouple on a bare board rather than a populated one measures a profile production will never run.

## Judging a measured profile
Compare the measured peak with the J-STD-020 classification temperature for the package size, and the measured time above liquidus with the J-STD-020 window of 60-150 s, narrowed by IPC-7530 guidance of 30-90 s and by the paste datasheet.

Use the worst-case measured point, not the oven profile or a single thermocouple. A peak above the classification temperature, or a time above liquidus outside the datasheet window, leaves the assembly non-compliant unless a separate qualification supports the exposure. Moisture sensitivity level, floor life and bake requirements from J-STD-020 also decide whether the component tolerates the exposure at all.

## Practical profile development
Start from the paste supplier's recommended profile and the component qualification limits. Run a dummy board with thermocouples at the expected worst-case locations, then adjust zones to move the measured soak, peak and cooling toward target while keeping the curve smooth.

Repeat the measurements after any change to stackup, panelisation, component placement or oven loading. Record the profile with board type, oven model, conveyor speed, zone setpoints and thermocouple locations so the result can be reproduced.

## Key takeaways
Measure on a populated board at the worst-case locations, because oven setpoints and bare-board runs both understate the real exposure.
Read the peak against the J-STD-020 classification for the package thickness and volume, not against a single remembered number.
Keep the paste time-above-liquidus window separate from the J-STD-020 tL classification window, since one runs the process and the other qualifies the package.

## Common mistakes
Quoting one peak temperature for every package on the board :: Read the classification temperature by thickness and volume, and check the largest part against it.
Judging time above liquidus from the oven display :: Take it from the measured curve crossing the alloy liquidus, at the worst-case thermocouple.
Slowing cooling without checking warpage and grain structure :: Verify the cooling rate against the paste datasheet and inspect the joints afterwards.

## Glossary
Time above liquidus :: The interval during which the solder alloy is fully molten.
Classification temperature :: The peak temperature J-STD-020 allows for a package, set by thickness and volume.
Wetting :: The spreading of molten solder over a metal surface to form a metallurgical bond.
Thermocouple :: A temperature sensor attached to the board to record the actual thermal history.
