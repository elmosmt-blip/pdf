## What aperture design controls
Aperture design decides how much paste reaches each pad and how cleanly it leaves the foil. The aperture has to be large enough to feed the joint and small enough to keep adjacent pads apart, which puts pad geometry, paste rheology, foil thickness, print speed, squeegee pressure and reflow behaviour into one balance.

For the process engineer the objective is narrower: an aperture that repeats its deposit and produces a predictable joint.

## Area reduction
Area reduction compares aperture area with pad area. A full-area aperture matches the pad; a reduced one is smaller, and fine-pitch designs reduce area to keep paste from bridging where slump or smear is likely. Too much reduction starves the joint and leaves insufficient solder.

A common starting point is 10 % to 20 %, verified against the paste datasheet, print inspection and reflow results.

## The area ratio rule
Area ratio is aperture opening area divided by sidewall area, and the sidewall is the aperture perimeter times the foil thickness. A low ratio puts more paste in contact with the wall than with the pad, and release suffers.

IPC-7525 stencil design guidelines commonly use 0.66 or higher as the target. It is a guideline, because paste type, foil finish and print settings move the real limit.

## The aspect ratio rule
Aspect ratio is the smaller aperture dimension divided by foil thickness, which describes how narrow the hole is relative to its depth. Low aspect ratio makes release hard on small apertures, and IPC-7525 commonly targets 1.5 or higher.

Check both ratios together. An aperture can pass one target and fail the other, and the failed parameter usually names the print defect.

## Foil thickness from volume and aperture
Foil thickness follows from the paste volume required and the aperture area available, since approximate volume is aperture area times thickness before losses. Thicker foil adds volume and lowers both ratios, so release gets harder; thinner foil releases better from small apertures and may not feed larger joints.

Common thicknesses run about 0.08 mm to 0.15 mm for fine pitch and 0.10 mm to 0.20 mm for larger components, with the final choice verified against the paste and equipment datasheets.

## Pitch-class guidance
Pitch class decides how much area reduction is needed and how exposed the print is to smear. In the 0.5 mm class a typical pad is around 0.30 mm square, where a small reduction controls bridging without killing release. Finer pitch means smaller apertures, thinner foil or tighter paste control; coarser pitch tolerates thicker foil and less reduction.

Check area ratio, aspect ratio, paste volume and print inspection rather than leaning on a single rule.

## Print risks from geometry
Geometry creates its own failure modes. Low area ratio gives incomplete release, so pads come up thin or empty; low aspect ratio leaves paste in the hole or smears it during lift-off. An aperture too large for the pitch bridges after print or in reflow, and one too small opens, weakens or tombstones the joint.

Thin foil cuts volume, and thick foil worsens release and raises smearing.

## Practical design sequence
Start from the pad drawing, pick an initial area reduction from pitch and paste behaviour, select the foil thickness that can deliver the volume, then calculate both ratios. If either falls below the IPC-7525 guideline, shrink the aperture within the pad boundary or reduce the foil.

Then verify with print inspection, release testing and reflow cross-sections, and document the aperture dimensions, foil thickness, both ratios, and the paste and equipment used.

## Key takeaways
Check area ratio and aspect ratio together before releasing a stencil, because an aperture can pass one and fail the other.
Trade foil thickness against release, since every millimetre of foil that adds volume also lowers both ratios.
Verify a new aperture with print inspection and reflow cross-sections, and record the numbers that produced the result.

## Common mistakes
Reducing aperture area to stop bridging without recalculating the ratios :: Recalculate area ratio and aspect ratio after every area change, and reduce foil if the ratio falls below 0.66.
Choosing foil thickness from habit rather than from the joint :: Derive the thickness from the volume the joint needs and the area the aperture has.
Documenting only the aperture dimensions on the drawing :: Record the foil thickness, both ratios and the paste used, or the design cannot be reproduced.

## Glossary
Area reduction :: The percentage by which the aperture area is made smaller than the pad area.
Area ratio :: Aperture opening area divided by the sidewall area of the aperture.
Aspect ratio :: The smaller aperture dimension divided by the foil thickness.
Pitch class :: The component lead spacing band that governs aperture and foil choices.
