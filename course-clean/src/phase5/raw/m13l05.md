## Node coverage and defect coverage
Node coverage counts the electrical nodes or nets a test method exercises; defect coverage estimates the share of likely defect modes it can actually detect.

A board can carry high node coverage and low defect coverage at the same time, if the test only checks connectivity at nominal conditions and misses marginal joints, parametric drift or thermal opens.

Reporting one number without the other hides the gap.

## Inspection detects physical nonconformities
Visual inspection, AOI and X-ray look for physical evidence: missing or wrong component, polarity, tombstoning, solder bridging, insufficient solder, foreign material and visible component damage.

They work on defects with an optical or radiographic signature and they do not prove electrical behaviour under power, temperature or signal loading. A joint can look perfect and be open, and a joint can look marginal and conduct.

## Test detects electrical and functional faults
In-circuit test, boundary scan and functional test apply electrical stimulus and measure the response.

They find opens, shorts, wrong component values, inverted orientation where it changes electrical behaviour, power faults and functional errors. They miss defects that are physically present and electrically invisible, such as a cosmetic solder issue or contamination that does not affect current.

## Coverage math needs a defect model
To estimate coverage, list the expected defect classes and assign each a detection probability per method.

Take the probabilities from your own process capability data, test logs or pilot builds, not from a vendor headline. Use a range rather than a point estimate, and check it against your paste and equipment datasheet.

Use SI units such as millimetre and micrometre for physical inspection limits, and ppm as the dimensionless rate for escape risk.

## Escape risk is coverage plus occurrence
Escape risk combines detection coverage with defect occurrence.

Where a defect class occurs at a known rate and a method detects a fraction of it, the residual escape rate is the occurrence rate multiplied by the undetected fraction. That gives a ppm figure for planning, never a guarantee: real escapes follow process drift, test program quality and intermittent defects.

## Where inspection ends and test starts
Inspection ends where the defect has no reliable physical signature, or the signature is too small, hidden or variable to inspect consistently.

Test starts where the question becomes whether the assembly behaves electrically or functionally. On hidden joints, X-ray or boundary scan bridges part of the gap, and neither replaces a test that exercises the node under representative conditions.

## Choosing a strategy defines the uncovered classes
A strategy built only on AOI leaves electrical opens under packages, shorts under components, wrong values inside tolerance and parametric faults uncovered.

A strategy built only on in-circuit test may leave cosmetic solder defects, intermittent joints, thermal opens and functional timing errors uncovered.

Document the chosen strategy with the defect classes it does not cover and the compensating controls used, so the gap is a decision rather than an accident.

## Key takeaways
Publish node coverage and defect coverage together, because the first number flatters any test that only checks connectivity.
Weight coverage by occurrence, since a method that catches a rare class adds little to the escape rate.
List the uncovered classes with the strategy, because the gap is where escapes come from.

## Common mistakes
Quoting a single coverage percentage for the whole board :: Report per defect class, because coverage is a property of a defect model.
Using vendor detection figures as your capability :: Measure on your own process data, since the same method behaves differently on different products.
Treating an AOI pass as an electrical pass :: Add a test that exercises the node, because visual signature and electrical behaviour are different questions.

## Glossary
Node coverage :: The fraction of electrical nodes a test method exercises.
Defect coverage :: The fraction of expected defect modes a method can detect, weighted by occurrence.
Escape :: A defective assembly that passes the inspection and test strategy and reaches the customer.
Detection probability :: The likelihood that a method reports a present defect of a given class.
