## Contact time and why it matters
Contact time or dwell is how long the board underside stays in the wave. Calculate it as t = L / v, where L is the effective contact length in mm and v the conveyor speed in mm/s.

Dwell governs heat transfer, flux activation, hole fill and solder penetration. Too little leaves icicles, poor wetting and incomplete fill; too much overheats the board, burns off flux, damages components and bridges.

## Effective contact length
Use the effective contact length, not the nozzle length. Turbulence, wave height, board clearance and capillary action in the holes extend the wetted zone beyond the nozzle.

Measure it with a witness board, thermal paper or a short test run with a marked board. On a dual-wave machine, record the contact length of each wave and the total residence in the combined zone.

## The dwell process window
Dwell windows are machine-, flux- and board-specific, and published practice is narrower than folklore suggests. A typical window is 2 to 4 s, lead-free processes commonly run 3 to 5 s, and only heavy boards or a chip-wave plus main-wave pair push contact beyond that, to about 6 s.

A single-wave window of 6 to 12 s would overheat thin boards and burn off flux long before it filled a barrel, so treat any figure above 6 s as something your own trial data has to justify. Verify against the flux and equipment datasheet and your trials.

Judge dwell by hole fill, wetting, residue and thermal stress rather than by clock time. A high-thermal-mass board may need longer dwell, while small components and thin boards may need less.

## Flux type and amount
Choose flux type from the board design and the cleaning capability. No-clean low-solids flux is common on mixed SMT and THT boards where residue is acceptable; water-soluble flux suits heavy oxidation or dense through-hole fill and requires cleaning.

Flux amount is specified as a deposition of flux solids in micrograms per square inch, measured with a fluxometer, with a typical recommended range of 500 to 1500 µg/in² of solids, about 0.8 to 2.3 g/m² (1 in² = 6.45 cm², 1 m² = 1550 in²). State the basis before comparing numbers: figures quoted per unit of liquid, for example 0.5 to 1.0 g/ft², are not comparable with solids-based figures and are several times larger.

Take the range from the flux datasheet and check it against the equipment datasheet. Too little flux gives poor wetting and dewetting; too much gives spatter, residue and solder balls.

## Preheat level
Preheat brings the board underside to a temperature that activates flux and removes solvent before wave contact.

Measure on the board underside near wave entry, not in the air. A common starting range is 90-130 °C, verified against your paste and equipment datasheet. Low preheat leaves solvent in the flux and causes solder balls or poor wetting; high preheat burns flux, oxidises pads and stresses components.

## Fog height and board clearance
Fog height, or wave crest height above the nozzle, sets how far the wave reaches toward the board. Board clearance is the gap between the crest and the board underside at entry.

A wave too high or a clearance too small lets the board meet the wave too early, which brings excessive dwell, bridging and board damage. A wave too low or a clearance too large stops the wave penetrating the holes, giving icicles and non-fill. Set height and clearance together, and check board bow and support.

## Setting sequence
Set flux type and amount first, then preheat, then wave height and board clearance, then conveyor speed.

Run a coupon with representative through-holes, pads and components. Adjust speed to bring dwell into the window, then adjust wave height or clearance to improve fill without pushing dwell out of it. Inspect the coupon against IPC-A-610 and record the settings.

## Key takeaways
Measure effective contact length with a witness board, because turbulence extends the wetted zone beyond the nozzle.
Bring dwell into the 2 to 5 s range by speed before adjusting wave height, since contact length is what the window describes.
State the flux basis in µg/in² of solids, because liquid-volume figures are several times larger and not comparable.

## Common mistakes
Calculating dwell from the nozzle length :: Use the measured effective contact length, or the dwell comes out short.
Slowing the conveyor to fill barrels :: Check wave height and clearance first, since a slow line overheats the board without filling.
Quoting a g/ft² liquid figure against a solids specification :: Convert to solids per unit area before comparing.

## Glossary
Dwell :: The time the board underside spends in contact with the solder wave.
Witness board :: A test board used to measure the effective contact length and wave footprint.
Fluxometer :: An instrument that measures flux solids deposition per unit area.
Board clearance :: The gap between the wave crest and the board underside at wave entry.
