## What the aperture calculator is for
An aperture calculator turns pad geometry, foil thickness and wall condition into release metrics you can review before fabrication: area ratio, aspect ratio and expected deposit volume. Those numbers let the process engineer decide whether the stencil will release paste and whether the drawing needs revision before anyone buys the foil.

## Area ratio and paste release
Area ratio is aperture opening area divided by the wall area the paste must leave. IPC-7525C recommends an area ratio above 0.66 for reliable release.

Values below that still work with some paste types, print speeds, squeegee pressures and foil finishes, but only after qualification against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Aspect ratio and aperture shape
Aspect ratio is aperture width divided by foil thickness, taken on the smaller dimension of a rectangular aperture because that dimension controls the release path. IPC-7525C recommends above 1.5. Thick foil on a small aperture pulls the ratio down and leaves paste sticking in the hole.

## Wall taper and aperture condition
Wall taper decides how easily paste leaves the aperture. IPC-7525C recommends 85 to 90 degrees from the horizontal, because a slight taper reduces the effective wall area.

Electropolishing, laser-drill condition, burrs and clogging all affect release too, and they belong on the drawing and in the measurement report rather than in the operator's memory.

## Expected deposit volume
Expected deposit volume starts at aperture area times foil thickness, with a taper correction when the bottom opening is smaller than the top. What actually transfers depends on transfer efficiency, which moves with paste rheology, print speed, squeegee pressure and foil finish — check it against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Drawing review before fabrication
Drawing review confirms aperture dimensions, tolerances, foil thickness, wall taper, fiducials, aperture-to-pad alignment, step features and the paste assumptions. It also confirms that the supplier's measurement method and sample size can actually verify the delivered stencil.

## Accept or reject from the measurement report
Accept against the issued specification, not against general guidance. A measured value outside the issued tolerance means the stencil is rejected or dispositioned by engineering, unless a qualified deviation exists.

Area ratio or aspect ratio below the qualified release limit triggers review even when every aperture dimension sits inside tolerance, because dimensions alone do not guarantee release.

## Key takeaways
Calculate area ratio, aspect ratio and expected volume before the stencil is ordered, and reject the drawing if either ratio misses its limit.
Judge a delivered stencil against the issued specification, including measured area ratio, not only the aperture dimensions.
Put wall taper, wall finish and cleanliness on the drawing and report, because release depends on them as much as on geometry.

## Common mistakes
Accepting a stencil because every dimension is in tolerance :: Check the measured area ratio against the issued release limit as well.
Comparing measured values to IPC-7525 general guidance instead of the drawing :: Use the issued specification, and document any limit the drawing leaves silent.
Skipping the taper correction in the volume estimate :: Apply it whenever the bottom opening is smaller, or the expected volume comes out optimistic.

## Glossary
Wall taper :: The angle of the aperture wall from the horizontal, recommended at 85 to 90 degrees by IPC-7525C.
Expected deposit volume :: Aperture volume corrected for taper and multiplied by transfer efficiency.
Measurement report :: The supplier's record of measured stencil characteristics against the issued drawing.
Qualified deviation :: A documented engineering approval to use a value outside the issued specification.
