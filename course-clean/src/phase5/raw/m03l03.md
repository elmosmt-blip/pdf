## Foil thickness sets volume and release risk
Foil thickness is the primary control on deposited paste volume: for a given aperture, volume is roughly aperture area times foil thickness. Thicker foil helps large pads, thermal relief and high-volume joints, and it raises the chance of slump, bridging and poor release on fine pitch.

Choose from the supplier's available range, commonly 0.075 mm to 0.200 mm, and check the choice against your paste rheology, your printer and your SPI capability.

## Aperture geometry must support release
Two release checks carry most of the work. Area ratio is aperture area divided by the product of aperture perimeter and foil thickness, and IPC-7525 commonly uses a minimum of 0.66 for reliable release. Aspect ratio is aperture width divided by foil thickness, with a common minimum of 1.5.

Both are design guidelines rather than guarantees, because paste type, wall finish and print speed decide the outcome too.

## Pad stack and pitch drive the thickness
Start from the tightest pitch on the board. Fine-pitch QFP, QFN and BGA pads usually need thinner foil or a local thin step so the aperture walls sit close enough to control paste shape. Larger pads and thermal pads may need more volume, which a thicker local step can supply where pitch is generous.

Where a board mixes both, decide up front whether one foil thickness can satisfy every feature or whether the design needs a step stencil.

## Aperture size is usually reduced from the pad
A full-pad aperture can overfill closely spaced pads and bridge, while a small one starves the joint and opens. Many processes reduce the aperture a few percent to tens of percent of the pad dimension, depending on paste slump, reflow profile and pad finish; your paste and equipment datasheet settles the number.

On rectangular pads keep the reduction balanced, so the deposit stays centred and favours no lead.

## Step stencils separate conflicting needs
A step stencil changes foil thickness locally: thin where the fine-pitch apertures are, thicker over large pads and thermal pads. Place the step where it does not disturb the support of nearby fine-pitch apertures, and keep the transition far enough from sensitive features to avoid paste leakage or uneven squeegee support.

Confirm the stencil maker's minimum step size, transition method and maximum step height. Those are process limits of that supplier, not universal values.

## Laser cut and wall finish
Laser cutting controls wall taper and finish. Rough or irregular walls hold paste, increase residue and make release less repeatable, while an aggressive taper changes the effective aperture at the top and the bottom of the foil.

Ask for the wall finish and taper the stencil maker can actually hold, such as electropolished or coated walls, and verify the result with SPI and print inspection. A finish does not fix a geometry problem.

## Selection sequence and verification
A practical order is: find the tightest pitch and the paste-volume requirements, choose a base foil thickness, size apertures to meet the IPC-7525 area-ratio and aspect-ratio guidelines while controlling volume, add a step only when one thickness cannot serve both ends of the board, then run a print trial with SPI.

Adjust aperture size before changing foil thickness where you can, because aperture changes iterate faster and cost less.

## Key takeaways
Set foil thickness from the tightest pitch first, then check the larger pads against it, because paste volume scales directly with thickness.
Read area ratio and aspect ratio against IPC-7525 before the stencil is cut, and treat either one failing as a design change, not a print tweak.
Add a step stencil only where a single thickness genuinely cannot serve both fine pitch and large thermal pads.

## Common mistakes
Thickening the whole foil to feed one thermal pad :: Use a local step and keep the fine-pitch area thin.
Assuming an electropolished wall rescues a failing area ratio :: Fix the aperture geometry first, then verify the finish with SPI.
Placing a step transition next to fine-pitch apertures :: Keep the transition clear of features that depend on even squeegee support.

## Glossary
Step stencil :: A stencil whose foil thickness changes in defined areas to give different paste volumes on one board.
Area ratio :: Aperture area divided by aperture perimeter times foil thickness.
Aspect ratio :: Aperture width divided by foil thickness on the narrowest dimension.
Wall taper :: The change in aperture size between the top and bottom of the laser-cut wall.
