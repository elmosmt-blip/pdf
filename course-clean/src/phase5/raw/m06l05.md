## Liquidus is the profile reference
SnPb eutectic paste is commonly associated with a liquidus of 183 C, while SAC305 is a near-eutectic alloy with a solidus of 217 °C and a liquidus of 219 C.

Those values come from the alloy and paste datasheet, not from J-STD-020, which supplies classification temperatures rather than melting data. The liquidus is where the alloy becomes fully molten, so time above liquidus is measured from the board thermocouple crossing that value, never from the oven setpoint.

## Alloy families set different thermal budgets
SnPb profiles usually run a lower peak, because the J-STD-020 classification temperature for SnPb eutectic is 235 °C for a package thinner than 2.5 mm with a volume below 350 mm³ and 220 °C in every other case.

SAC305 profiles run higher: 260 °C for small packages, 250 °C for large and 245 °C for very large at 1.6-2.5 mm thickness, where the full table is thickness times volume, every volume allows 260 °C below 1.6 mm, and above 2.5 mm the limits fall to 250 °C small and 245 °C large and very large. The practical target peak comes from paste wetting, component rating and board stress, then gets checked against the classification limit.

## Ramp and soak control flux and gradients
IPC-7530B gives typical guidance such as 1-3 °C/s ramp and a soak that reduces thermal gradients.

Heavy boards, large ground planes and dense components need a slower ramp or a longer soak, so the coldest joint reaches liquidus without overheating the hottest. Verify the chosen ramp and soak against the paste and oven datasheets.

## Time above liquidus is a joint-quality window
IPC-7530B gives typical guidance of 30-90 s above liquidus. The 60-120 s figure often quoted beside it is the soak, ts, the time from Tsmin to Tsmax in the J-STD-020 classification profile, not the time above liquidus.

That window allows wetting and intermetallic formation without exhausting the flux or growing intermetallic to excess. Heavy thermal mass may need longer above liquidus, but the limit gets checked against the paste datasheet and component moisture sensitivity.

## Peak temperature is a ceiling, not a target
A SnAgCu peak of 245 °C may be acceptable if the measured board temperature stays below the J-STD-020 classification temperature for the package size — 260 °C small, 250 °C large, 245 °C very large on the 1.6-2.5 mm row, with above 2.5 mm falling to 250 °C small and 245 °C large and very large — and the paste datasheet supports it.

Measure the peak at the joint rather than in the oven air, because board thermal mass and thermocouple placement both shift the apparent value.

## Nitrogen changes oxidation, not the liquidus
Nitrogen reflow cuts oxide formation on paste and pads, which can improve wetting and may allow a lower peak or a shorter time above liquidus. It does not move the SnAgCu liquidus of 219 °C (217 °C is the solidus) or the SnPb liquidus of 183 C.

Verify oxygen level, gas flow and chamber uniformity, because nitrogen also changes heat transfer and profile repeatability.

## Build the profile from measured board data
For a given board thermal mass, attach thermocouples to representative joints, ground planes and component leads. Record ramp rate, time above liquidus and peak.

Compare the measured values with IPC-7530B guidance and the IPC/JEDEC J-STD-020 classification limits, then adjust zones until the coldest joint meets wetting requirements without the hottest joint exceeding its limit.

## Key takeaways
Read the liquidus from the paste datasheet for the alloy in use, because J-STD-020 classifies packages and does not give melting data.
Keep the alloy's thermal budget separate from its classification ceiling, since the second bounds the first rather than replacing it.
Re-profile after switching to nitrogen, because the gas changes heat transfer even when the liquidus stays the same.

## Common mistakes
Quoting a single peak for both SnPb and SAC305 assemblies :: Check the J-STD-020 classification temperature for the alloy and package, which differ substantially.
Measuring time above liquidus from setpoints :: Take it from the board thermocouple crossing the alloy liquidus.
Treating the 60-120 s figure as time above liquidus :: That is the J-STD-020 soak window ts, not the molten time.

## Glossary
Solidus :: The temperature below which an alloy is fully solid.
Near-eutectic :: An alloy close to the composition with the lowest melting point in its system.
Nitrogen reflow :: Reflow in a nitrogen atmosphere that limits oxide formation during melting.
Classification temperature :: The peak temperature J-STD-020 permits for a package, by thickness and volume.
