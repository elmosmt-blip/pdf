## Visual inspection
Visual inspection, with or without magnification, catches defects that are exposed: missing or wrong components, polarity errors, tombstoned chips, visible bridges on exposed pads, insufficient or excess solder on through-hole leads, mechanical damage, contamination and flux residue.

It suits gross assembly errors and cosmetic acceptability under IPC-A-610, and it cannot reliably judge joints hidden under packages, bottom terminations, BGA balls, QFN wettable flanks, stacked layers, internal voids or barrel fill that no eye can reach.

## AOI
Automated optical inspection uses cameras, lighting and image analysis on exposed top and bottom features. It finds component presence, polarity, orientation, wrong and missing parts, tombstoning, visible bridges, coplanarity problems and some fillet or wettable flank features where lighting and 3D height mapping are good enough.

AOI is fast for inline screening, and it misses what sits under a package, behind a tall component, inside a BGA or QFN termination or down a barrel. Check lighting, angle and resolution against the equipment datasheet.

## 2D X-ray
A 2D X-ray produces a transmission projection and takes over where optics fail. It shows BGA, QFN, PLCC and CSP termination geometry, missing or collapsed balls, opens, shorts, misalignment, voids and barrel fill.

Because the image superimposes layers, void size, depth and bridging location stay ambiguous, and thin wettable flanks are hard to judge where traces or neighbouring balls overlap. Use it to screen hidden joints, with magnification and detector resolution verified against the equipment datasheet.

## Laminar and 3D X-ray
Laminar or 3D X-ray reconstructs slices or volumes, which gives depth discrimination no projection can. Use it to locate a void, estimate its size, inspect stacked packages, separate overlapping BGA rows, judge QFN wettable flanks or evaluate barrel fill in dense boards, and it supports the IPC-7095 guidance on hidden BGA features.

The costs are cycle time, capital, reconstruction artefacts and sensitivity to calibration, so reserve it for cases where 2D X-ray or AOI cannot settle the question, and do not run it as a routine replacement for 2D screening.

## Boundary scan
Boundary scan, usually JTAG, uses scan cells inside devices to observe and drive package pins. It detects opens and shorts on nets connected to scan-capable pins, which makes it valuable on dense BGA and QFN assemblies where optics are limited.

It does not inspect wetting, component value, non-scan nets, power and ground nets, analog nodes, memory arrays or joints under non-scan devices. It needs design-for-test access, a working scan chain and test vectors that exercise the nets you care about.

## Choosing from the defect and the feature
Match the method to what must be seen. Visual or AOI handles exposed top-side defects, visible through-hole leads, polarity, missing parts and visible bridges, and bottom-side AOI takes the underside.

2D X-ray covers hidden BGA, QFN, PLCC, CSP and barrel features. Laminar or 3D X-ray resolves void size, depth, stacking and overlapping geometry. Boundary scan covers opens and shorts on scan-capable nets in dense packages, combined with physical inspection for non-scan nets and solder quality.

## Practical limits and acceptance
No single method covers every defect. Optical methods depend on lighting, angle, component height and reflectivity; X-ray on magnification, resolution, focus, orientation and reconstruction; boundary scan on chain integrity and coverage.

Acceptance follows the customer-specified IPC-A-610 class and the relevant design guidance such as IPC-7351 and IPC-7525. Take practice-based limits from your equipment and paste datasheets rather than assuming one value.

## Key takeaways
Choose the method from what must be seen, because an optical screen cannot answer a question about a joint under a package.
Combine electrical and physical inspection on dense packages, since boundary scan proves connectivity and not wetting.
Check the resolution and coverage limits of the chosen method before accepting its verdict.

## Common mistakes
Relying on AOI to clear a BGA :: Use X-ray for hidden terminations, because AOI cannot see under the package.
Treating boundary scan as full joint inspection :: It proves opens and shorts on scan nets and says nothing about wetting or voids.
Using 2D X-ray to size a void :: Use laminar or 3D X-ray, because projection overlays layers and distorts depth.

## Glossary
Boundary scan :: An electrical test method that uses scan cells inside devices to drive and observe package pins.
Wettable flank :: A plated side feature on a QFN that lets optical inspection judge the fillet.
Laminar X-ray :: Tomographic X-ray that reconstructs a slice through the assembly.
Projection image :: A 2D X-ray image in which every layer contributes to the same picture.
