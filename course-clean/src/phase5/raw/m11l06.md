## Scope and purpose
This practice asks you to rework a solder joint, document the operation, and classify the result against the IPC-7711/7721 rework and repair requirements at the acceptance class the product specifies.

The product owner normally states the required IPC-A-610 class, Class 2 for instance, and the reworked joint must meet that class unless an approved deviation exists.

## Thermal budget before heat
Record the budget before the iron goes near the joint: allowed rework cycles, peak temperature, dwell, preheat temperature and cooling method, taken from the product specification, IPC-7711/7721 and the paste, component and equipment datasheets.

Use the lowest thermal input that produces wetting, and check it against your datasheet, because alloy, board finish, mass and nearby components all move the working window. A budget written after the fact is a description, not a control.

## Preparation and contamination control
Inspect the joint, clean the area, and remove old solder, flux residue and moisture-sensitive residues with approved materials.

Confirm that pad, land and nearby components are not lifted, cracked or delaminated. If damage is already there, stop and evaluate a repair under IPC-7711/7721 instead of carrying on with rework, which would only deepen it.

## Performing the rework
Apply heat in a controlled way, with hot gas, a soldering iron or a local rework station as approved.

Add only the solder and flux needed to wet the joint, then let it solidify without disturbance. Record the tool, the tip or nozzle, the temperature setpoint, the measured peak temperature, the dwell, the number of thermal cycles and any cleaning performed.

## Documentation of the class claim
A rework record identifies the assembly, serial number, date, operator, station, original defect, action taken, thermal data, materials used and the post-rework inspection result.

It also states the required IPC-A-610 class and whether the joint meets IPC-7711/7721 rework or repair acceptance for that class, because the record is the evidence behind the class claim.

## Classifying the reworked joint
Inspect against the IPC-A-610 acceptance criteria for the required class, then confirm the method is acceptable under IPC-7711/7721.

A joint that fails becomes a repair candidate, a deviation candidate or scrap under the approved process. Rework is never automatically acceptable just because the inspector did it.

## Failure modes to watch
The usual problems are insufficient wetting, lifted pads, thermal damage to components or laminate, excess flux residue, disturbance of an adjacent joint, and mechanical stress from component removal.

Record each with evidence and disposition it against IPC-7711/7721 and IPC-A-610, so the failure is traceable to a cause and a decision rather than to a retry.

## Key takeaways
Write the thermal budget before applying heat, because a budget recorded afterwards cannot constrain the operation.
Count every heat application as a cycle, including the iron touch-up, since the allowance is spent in exposures and not in passes.
Record the class and the evidence with the rework, so the acceptance claim can be defended later.

## Common mistakes
Treating the iron touch-up as outside the cycle count :: Count it, because it is a thermal exposure like any other.
Reworking on top of a lifted pad :: Stop and evaluate a repair, since more heat makes the damage worse.
Claiming acceptance without the thermal record :: Record peak temperature and dwell, because the class claim rests on that evidence.

## Glossary
Touch-up :: A short iron pass used to correct a joint after the main rework heating.
Deviation :: An approved departure from the specified acceptance criteria.
Disposition :: The recorded decision to accept, repair, rework or scrap an assembly.
Thermal cycle :: One heating and cooling exposure counted against the allowed rework allowance.
