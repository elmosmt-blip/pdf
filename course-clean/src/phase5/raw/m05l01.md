## What the placement stage controls
Placement moves components from feeders, trays or rails onto the board and fixes their starting position before reflow or insertion. Accuracy is how close the commanded position is to the true board location, repeatability is how tightly repeated placements cluster, and throughput is the rate of valid placements.

The three interact. Faster motion, weak vacuum, worn feeders or an unverified program all turn a repeatable machine into a systematic defect source.

## Nozzle selection
Pick a nozzle that reaches the component without touching adjacent parts, feeder walls or the board. For SMT, match the tip diameter to the component top area and mass; for THT, match the gripper or insertion nozzle to the lead diameter and body clearance.

Check nozzle ID, vacuum port size, tip length and clearance against the component drawing, the feeder datasheet and the machine nozzle map.

## Pick height
Pick height is the nozzle Z position where it contacts the component. Too high and the pick is missed or the feeder wears; too low and the component is pushed into its pocket, the tape distorts, or a brittle part cracks.

Set the initial value from component height, feeder pocket depth, tape thickness and nozzle tip length, then confirm it with a dry run and microscope inspection.

## Placement force
Placement force has to seat the component in paste, or hold a THT lead, without crushing the part, displacing paste or bending leads. On SMT it is usually a small controlled value above component weight and paste resistance; on THT it follows lead diameter, hole size, plating and insertion speed.

Verify the value against the component maximum force, the nozzle contact area and the machine's force capability.

## Accuracy, repeatability and throughput
A machine can be repeatable and still inaccurate when axes, vision or nozzle offsets are uncalibrated. Calibration and compensation fix accuracy; stable feeders, clean nozzles and a steady air supply protect repeatability.

Throughput counts only while the first-article placement stays inside the process window, because speed that adds mispicks or misalignment buys rework and scrap.

## Program and setup verification
Before production, verify the BOM, component library, nozzle map, feeder setup, pick and place coordinates, rotation, skip logic, vision settings, stencil alignment and panel support.

Run a low-speed dry cycle or first article, inspect the first board, and compare measured placement offsets with the program. Record the result so a later defect can be traced to setup, program or machine condition.

## Calibration status
Confirm that machine axes, the vision system, the nozzle changer, feeder alignment and any force or torque sensor hold current calibration under the equipment OEM and the plant quality system.

A missing or expired certificate produces consistent offsets, rotation errors, mispicks or force drift. If the certificate is absent, expired or outside its scope, stop the setup and escalate before production.

## Key takeaways
Set pick height from the component, pocket and nozzle geometry, then confirm it on a dry run rather than trusting the program.
Keep placement force above the seating need and below the component's stated maximum, and verify the nozzle contact area behind that number.
Check calibration scope before the setup, because an expired certificate explains offsets that no programming change will fix.

## Common mistakes
Choosing a nozzle that fits the component body but not its clearances :: Check adjacent parts, feeder walls and board clearance, not just the tip diameter.
Correcting an offset with program coordinates when calibration has expired :: Verify and renew calibration first, then measure the offset again.
Running at full speed straight from a new program :: Prove the first article at low speed and compare measured offsets before releasing production.

## Glossary
Accuracy :: How close the commanded placement position is to the true board location.
Repeatability :: How tightly repeated placements cluster around their own mean.
Pick height :: The nozzle Z position at which it contacts the component in the feeder or tray.
Nozzle map :: The machine table that pairs each component with its nozzle and vacuum setting.
