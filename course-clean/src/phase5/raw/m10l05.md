## Purpose of selective validation
Selective soldering exists because a full wave would overheat nearby SMT or temperature-sensitive THT parts.

Validation proves two things at once: the joint is mechanically and electrically sound, and the neighbours stay inside their allowed thermal budget. Use IPC-A-610 for visual acceptance, IPC-7711/7721 for rework and repair, and IPC-7530 for thermal process control where it applies.

## Building the selective program
Start from the board layout and define each joint as a solder target.

Order the joints so heat-sensitive parts go last where possible, and so the nozzle approaches without disturbing adjacent components. Set dwell per joint from the time needed to wet pad and hole, then add a short margin for paste variability.

One pass covers simple through-hole joints; multiple passes come in only when pad, hole or paste volume needs more heat. Verify against your paste, nozzle and equipment datasheet.

## Estimating heat exposure
Estimate exposure from preheat, wave or jet temperature, contact time, board thickness, copper area, lead mass and standoff.

The first estimate is a measurement: record the temperature rise at the nearest sensitive part with a thermocouple or thermal probe during a dry run, then repeat with paste and a sacrificial board.

Where direct measurement is impossible, use a conservative model based on thermal mass and contact time, and check it against the equipment datasheet.

## Mitigation and verification
Mitigate by reducing preheat, lowering jet temperature within paste limits, shortening dwell, increasing standoff, shielding the sensitive part, using a smaller nozzle, or moving the sensitive joint later in the sequence.

Verification covers a thermal profile at the sensitive part, visual inspection after cooling, and functional or electrical test where the part is thermally sensitive. Document the profile and the accepted program revision.

## Cross-section validation
Cross-sections answer what the eye cannot: joint geometry, hole fill, intermetallic formation and crack risk.

Prepare the sample so the section passes through pad, hole and lead, then inspect for wetting, voiding, cracks and excessive intermetallic. Accept against IPC-A-610 and repair per IPC-7711/7721. Take sections from representative boards rather than from one ideal sample.

## Pull test validation
Pull testing suits THT leads and selected SMT terminations where mechanical strength is the concern.

Fix the board, pull the lead perpendicular at a controlled rate, and record force and failure mode. Acceptance rests on the specified minimum strength or a validated process capability, not on a single pass or fail sample. Follow the applicable component and board standards, and document sample size, temperature and failure mode.

## Documentation
Documentation carries board revision, component placement, program file, nozzle, paste lot, preheat and jet settings, dwell per joint, pass count, thermal profile, cross-section results, pull-test results and any deviation.

When a defect appears, record the symptom, the suspected cause, the corrective action and the revalidation evidence, so the program stays auditable and repeatable.

## Key takeaways
Validate the neighbour and the joint in the same run, because selective soldering protects one by risking the other.
Measure the sensitive part on a sacrificial board before release, since a thermal estimate only screens.
Record the program revision with the profile and the section, so a later change can be traced to its evidence.

## Common mistakes
Verifying only the joint that was soldered :: Profile the nearest sensitive part, which is what the process was chosen to protect.
Accepting a pull test on one sample :: Use a documented sample size and the failure mode, not a single pass.
Releasing a program after a mitigation without re-profiling :: Repeat the profile, because the mitigation changed the thermal path.

## Glossary
Sacrificial board :: A production-equivalent board used for thermal or destructive testing.
Pull test :: A mechanical test that measures the force needed to separate a joint.
Thermal budget :: The temperature and time a part may tolerate without damage.
Program revision :: The versioned record of path, dwell, pass and process settings.
