## Frame the change request as a contract
An ECN or DCR states what is wrong, what is being changed, and how acceptance will be proven.

It identifies affected part numbers, drawings, BOM levels, routings, test programs and documents. For SMT and through-hole work, add board revision, panelisation, feeder or insertion head, stencil, wave or selective solder, and inspection method.

Write it so design, manufacturing, quality and purchasing can all read it without a translator.

## Write the problem with evidence
The problem statement carries defect mode, location, occurrence, detection method and consequence.

Use measurable terms: solder voiding on QFN pads detected by X-ray, tombstoning at a specific feeder, lead seating error at a connector row. State sample size, lot, date, equipment and operator where they matter.

Where acceptance criteria are needed, cite IPC-A-610 and verify the applicable class against the product specification.

## Show impact before proposing
The impact section covers electrical, mechanical, thermal, assembly, test, reliability, inventory and qualification effects.

For a land-pattern change, cite IPC-7351 for footprint geometry and IPC-7095 for BGA. For through-hole work, cover lead forming, seating, wave dwell, clearance and interference with adjacent components.

Say whether existing inventory can be consumed, reworked or scrapped, and who pays for it.

## Make the proposal unambiguous
The proposal specifies old and new values, the drawing revision, the BOM change, process parameter changes and work instruction updates.

For paste changes, state stencil thickness, aperture dimensions and paste type, verified against the paste and printer datasheet. For through-hole work, state lead finish, seating tolerance and wave or selective profile limits.

Include traceability, first-article requirements and the list of documents that change.

## List alternatives and rejection reasons
Consider at least two alternatives: process adjustment, component substitution, layout change, or test change.

Compare technical feasibility, cost, schedule, inventory and risk. A rejected alternative states why, for example a process window too narrow, no qualified component, insufficient test coverage, or an unacceptable rework burden.

This section is what defends the request against the objection that it was chosen before the analysis.

## Quantify risk and residual risk
Use a simple risk table: failure mode, severity, occurrence, detection, mitigation, residual risk.

For rework and repair limits cite IPC-7711/7721, and for soldering process qualification cite IPC J-STD-001. State the controls: SPC, AOI, X-ray, in-circuit test, functional test, environmental stress screening.

Do not claim zero risk. State the residual risk you accept and who accepts it.

## Define verification and sign-off
Verification includes the sample plan, acceptance criteria, data format and disposition.

Measure dimensional changes with calibrated tools. Inspect solder joints by cross-section or X-ray against IPC-A-610 where required. Where the change affects joint stress, define the thermal cycling or vibration test.

Close the ECN or DCR only when the data, the updated documents and the inventory disposition are all complete.

## Key takeaways
State what changes and how acceptance is proven, because a change request without a proof is an opinion.
Show impact and alternatives before the proposal, since reviewers approve what they can see was compared.
Name the residual risk and its owner, because a claim of zero risk invites rejection.

## Common mistakes
Sending a change request that names only the desired outcome :: Give old and new values, because the implementer needs the difference.
Omitting the inventory and document impact :: List them, since a change that leaves stock stranded gets deferred.
Verifying with an unstated sample plan :: State the sample and the criterion, because acceptance without a plan is a judgement.

## Glossary
DCR :: Design change request, the engineering request that initiates a change before formal approval.
First article :: The first unit built to a changed design, inspected in full.
Residual risk :: The risk that remains after mitigation, accepted by a named owner.
Panelisation :: The arrangement of boards on a production panel, which a design change can affect.
