## Purpose of profile validation
Profile validation checks whether the measured thermal history at the joint matches the paste, component and board constraints. Put thermocouples on representative joints, not only in the oven air.

Compare the measured curve with the paste datasheet and the component moisture sensitivity limits from the applicable IPC moisture sensitivity guidance.

## Review the profile by zones
A reflow profile is reviewed zone by zone. Preheat controls solvent evaporation and prevents thermal shock. Soak activates flux and equalises board temperature. Reflow creates wetting and intermetallic formation. Cooling controls solidification and residual stress.

Each zone gives a different lever, so naming the zone first keeps the adjustment small.

## Evidence needed for validation
Validation records the location of every thermocouple, the board orientation, the carrier or pallet, the oven setpoints and the measured peak temperature.

A profile is invalid if the thermocouple is loose, the joint is unrepresentative, or the measurement comes from a heavily loaded board with no check against a lightly loaded one.

## Measurement quality
Attachment affects the result. Use a high-temperature adhesive or a solder point on the joint, and confirm the sensor is not lifted by conveyor vibration.

Measure several joints across the board, because thermal mass and airflow create gradients. On a multi-zone oven, record the measured curve rather than trusting the setpoints.

## Linking defect signatures to zones
When a signature points to a zone, choose the smallest change that tests the hypothesis. Poor wetting makes the reflow zone the first candidate. A joint that stays cold needs more energy in preheat or soak. Tombstoning points to preheat and soak being too aggressive, or to uneven cooling.

## Bounded change control
Bounded change control means changing one profile variable, recording the old and new values, and verifying with measurement and inspection.

If time above liquidus is short, raise the peak slightly or slow the cooling, then confirm the joint still meets the acceptance criteria in the applicable IPC acceptance guidance.

## Documentation for traceability
Document the measured profile, the thermocouple map, the paste lot, the board stackup, the oven recipe, the change reason, the verification data and the disposition.

That record makes the change auditable and stops the next shift repeating the trial and error. Where the change touches moisture-sensitive components, check the applicable IPC moisture sensitivity guidance before releasing the recipe.

## Recipe adjustment versus qualification
Change control separates a recipe adjustment from a process qualification.

A small bounded adjustment is proven by profile measurement and joint inspection. A larger change — new paste, new board design, new oven — needs a formal qualification with statistical confidence and acceptance evidence.

## Key takeaways
Validate at the joint with a documented thermocouple map, because a loose or unrepresentative sensor invalidates the run.
Change one profile variable and verify it, so the result can be attributed and the recipe stays inside its limits.
Separate a bounded adjustment from a qualification, since a new paste or oven cannot be proven by a profile alone.

## Common mistakes
Accepting a profile measured with a detached thermocouple :: Confirm sensor attachment and location before trusting any curve.
Treating a new paste as a recipe adjustment :: Run a formal qualification with statistical evidence for a material change.
Changing the peak and the cooling rate together :: Change one variable at a time, then re-measure both time above liquidus and peak.

## Glossary
Thermocouple map :: The record of where each thermocouple was attached and what it measured.
Recipe adjustment :: A small, bounded change to oven settings verified by profile and inspection.
Process qualification :: Formal evidence that a process meets requirements under defined conditions.
Bounded change control :: Changing one variable at a time and verifying the outcome before the next change.
