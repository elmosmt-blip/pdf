## Cleanliness testing as a decision tool
A cleanliness result is read against the defect risk of the assembly, not only against a generic limit.

On coated electronics the real question is whether residue, ionic contamination, particulate, flux or masking material can cause corrosion, insulation failure, solderability loss, coating defects or an ESD handling problem. A result that is acceptable on a low-density through-hole board can be unacceptable on fine-pitch SMT, bottom-terminated components or high-impedance analogue circuits.

The pass or rework criterion therefore combines the applicable standard, the customer requirement and the process capability behind it.

## What the tests measure
Visual inspection under magnification finds visible residue, flux, solder balls, masking bleed and particulate.

Solvent extraction conductivity or ion chromatography estimates ionic contamination, reported as sodium chloride equivalent or as individual ions such as chloride, sulfate, nitrate and bromide. Surface insulation resistance measures electrical leakage and can reveal contamination that no eye will find.

Cleanliness patch tests, ROSE-type, give a rapid ionic estimate and can miss non-ionic residue, rosin, polymers and masking films, so no single test answers the whole question.

## Choosing a pass or rework criterion
A practical criterion states the test method, the area sampled, the acceptance limit, the inspection magnification and the action when the limit is exceeded.

For example, a board may pass if no visible residue is present at the magnification IPC-A-610 requires for the product class and if ionic contamination is below the limit agreed for that assembly and coating system.

On a marginal result, rework is usually better than retest where the residue type is known to interfere with coating adhesion or wetting. Where the result is clean but the coating shows fish-eye, the contamination lives on the surface or in the coating itself, not in the extract.

## Residue types and coating consequences
Flux residue survives under components, in thermal relief pads and under bottom-terminated packages.

Rosin-based residue resists aqueous cleaning and causes poor wetting or coating adhesion. No-clean residue may be electrically acceptable in some designs and still create coating defects when the coating solvent reactivates or lifts it.

Masking tape adhesive, silicone release agents and cured masking material leave low-surface-energy films that cause fish-eye or dewetting, and particulate from paste, cutting, grinding or handling creates coating defects and local corrosion sites.

## White residue and corrosion
White residue often appears after cleaning, drying or humidity exposure, and it may be a salt, a flux reaction product, a mineral deposit or a corrosion product.

Where the residue is conductive or hygroscopic it lowers insulation resistance and promotes corrosion under the coating. Corrosion may start as discolouration, green or white deposits, lifted metallisation, or an intermittent electrical fault.

A coating hides the early stage, which is why pre-coating cleanliness and drying matter so much. White residue found after coating sends you back to whether it formed before coating, migrated through, or came from the coating.

## Fish-eye and dewetting
Fish-eye appears as small circular areas where the coating fails to wet the substrate; dewetting is the coating pulling back and leaving thin or uncovered regions.

Both usually trace to low-surface-energy contamination: silicone, mould release, adhesive residue, skin oils or certain masking materials. They also occur when substrate surface energy is too low for the coating, or when viscosity, solvent balance or drying schedule is wrong.

The first corrective action is to find the contamination source and remove it, then verify by coating a coupon of the same lot.

## Coating and masking strategy
Choose the coating from the protection required: acrylic where rework matters, urethane for chemical and moisture resistance, silicone for thermal cycling and humidity, epoxy for hard chemical resistance where rework is rare, and parylene for pinhole-free coverage at the cost of vacuum deposition and special masking.

Masking protects connectors, test points, heatsink interfaces, optical surfaces and mounting areas, and must survive cleaning, cure and assembly without bleeding, lifting or leaving residue.

## Cleaning, rework and assembly checks
Before releasing a coating and masking plan, confirm three things: cleaning does not attack the mask, the mask leaves no residue after removal, and the coating does not swell or soften in the rework solvent.

For rework, confirm that the coating can be removed locally without lifting adjacent coating or damaging component bodies. For assembly, confirm that coated surfaces do not foul press-fit connectors, screw threads, thermal pads, snap fits or grounding contacts.

Where an area must stay conductive, mask it or use a selected removal method, and verify continuity afterwards.

## ESD in coating and masking
Conformal coating changes surface resistance and can affect handling of ESD-sensitive assemblies.

Some coatings insulate enough to let charge accumulate on the surface unless the assembly is handled inside the ESD program. Masking tapes can generate charge when they are peeled.

For ESD-sensitive assemblies, verify the surface resistance of the coated area and of the handling path, use ESD-safe masking and removal procedures, and where the design needs dissipative behaviour, confirm that the coating and mask do not leave isolated insulating islands near sensitive devices.

## Key takeaways
Set the criterion from the assembly's defect risk, because the same reading passes one board and fails another.
Test for the contaminant you actually fear, since a patch test cannot see a non-ionic film.
Chase fish-eye to its source on the surface, because the extract can read clean while the coating refuses to wet.

## Common mistakes
Accepting a board on the extract value alone :: Inspect and coat a coupon, since adhesion is the property that fails.
Reworking without finding the residue source :: Identify the process that left it, because cleaning the same way will leave it again.
Peeling ESD-safe masking tape without grounding yourself :: Handle inside the ESD program, since peeling generates charge.

## Glossary
Fish-eye :: A circular area where coating fails to wet the surface, leaving bare substrate.
Dewetting :: Coating pulling back from an area and leaving it thinly covered or uncovered.
Patch test :: A rapid extraction test that estimates ionic contamination over a defined area.
Dissipative :: A surface resistance range that bleeds charge away slowly enough to protect sensitive devices.
