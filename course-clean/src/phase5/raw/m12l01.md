## Residue is not one thing
Residue is any unwanted material left on a board after assembly: flux solids, solder paste residues, no-clean flux polymer films, fingerprints, dust, handling oils and ionic salts.

It matters because it holds moisture, supports electrochemical migration, changes surface energy, interferes with coating adhesion and raises contact resistance. In coating and ESD stages it can also create localised charge paths or blunt the effect of ionised air blowers.

Treat residue as a process defect with a source, a mechanism and a verification method, not as a cosmetic issue.

## Ionic residue, ROSE and what it does not prove
Ionic residue is contamination that dissolves in water and can carry current once a thin water film forms.

ROSE tests measure soluble ionic contamination as sodium chloride equivalent, usually with IPC-TM-650 methods and the cleanliness requirements that IPC J-STD-001 references.

A low ROSE value is useful and it does not prove the board is free of organic, hydrophobic or particulate residue. A board can pass its ROSE limit and still carry a no-clean flux film that absorbs moisture, lowers surface insulation resistance or stops the coating wetting. Use ROSE as a gate, and pair it with visual inspection, SIR or CAF testing where the product has fine pitch, high-impedance nodes or long-term reliability requirements.

## SIR and CAF: residue becomes a reliability failure
Surface insulation resistance measures how well a surface blocks current between conductors under humidity and bias, commonly evaluated with IPC-TM-650 methods.

Low SIR can come from ionic residue, absorbed moisture, flux films or contamination bridging fine-pitch lands. Conductive anodic filament is a related failure in which copper migrates along glass fibres in the laminate, accelerated by moisture, bias and residue that lowers local insulation resistance, and it is normally screened with IPC-TM-650 CAF methods.

SIR and CAF are different tests that say the same thing: the surface or the laminate is not electrically stable under the intended service condition.

## Contact failure: residue at the interface
Contact failure happens when residue changes the metal-to-metal interface at edge connectors, spring contacts, press-fit pins or test points.

Thin organic films raise contact resistance, cause intermittent opens, or promote fretting corrosion by changing lubricity and moisture retention. Gold plating tolerates some contamination without being immune: particulate and ionic residue still cause intermittent contact or corrosion under the contact.

Cleaning chemistry must suit the plating, the elastomers and the connectors. On ESD-sensitive assemblies, cleaning and handling follow ANSI/ESD S20.20 or IEC 61340-5-1, because a clean board can still be damaged by uncontrolled charge transfer.

## Selecting cleaning method and chemistry
Select cleaning by residue type, assembly geometry and downstream constraints.

Water-soluble flux usually wants aqueous cleaning with a saponifier or alkaline cleaner and a thorough deionised water rinse. No-clean flux may need a semi-aqueous solvent, a low-surface-tension aqueous cleaner, or a solvent rinse followed by an aqueous wash, depending on how much residue remains and whether reflow has turned it into a polymer film.

Rosin residues often respond to alcohol or solvent cleaning, and the solvent choice has to consider flammability, material compatibility and environmental permits. Verify cleaner concentration, temperature and rinse quality against your paste and equipment datasheet.

For bottom-terminated packages, high-aspect-ratio gaps and under-component spaces, the method has to wet, penetrate and rinse.

That rules out a spray that only reaches the visible surface and points towards agitation, vacuum-assisted cleaning, or ultrasonic cleaning where the assembly tolerates it.

A cleaner that cannot reach the residue cannot remove it, and the board leaves the line looking clean while the gap stays contaminated. That reach is part of method selection, not a separate step.

## MSL and ESD constraints on cleaning
Moisture-sensitive devices need moisture control before and after cleaning.

Where an assembly has been exposed beyond the floor life that IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033 assigns, bake it before reflow or rework according to the MSL level and the product specification. Cleaning can reintroduce moisture, so the drying, bake and reflow windows need control.

For ESD-sensitive work, use grounded surfaces, wrist straps, ionisers and ESD-safe containers as ANSI/ESD S20.20 or IEC 61340-5-1 requires, and avoid high-velocity dry air and uncontrolled plastic film that generate charge. Where cleaning happens inside an ESD-controlled area, confirm that the equipment, baskets and transport materials suit the ESD program.

## Interpreting cleanliness results and deciding rework
Decide pass or rework against a written criterion, never against operator opinion.

The criterion may come from IPC J-STD-001, a customer specification, a reliability plan, or an IPC-TM-650 method applied to a coupon or an assembly. A typical decision tree reads: visual residue means rework; ROSE above the limit means rework; a failed SIR or CAF minimum means rework even where ROSE passes.

On a marginal result, repeat the test on a fresh coupon or a defined area and document the sampling location. When reworking, find the residue source, adjust chemistry or rinse, retest and record the result; if the rework touches components, follow IPC-7711/7721 and re-evaluate MSL exposure.

## Key takeaways
Read ROSE as a gate rather than a verdict, because it measures ionic species and says nothing about a hydrophobic flux film.
Let the failed SIR result decide when ROSE passes, since impedance under bias is closer to the service condition.
Pick the chemistry from the residue and the geometry, because a cleaner that cannot reach under a bottom termination cannot remove what is there.

## Common mistakes
Accepting a board on the ROSE number alone :: Test SIR where the product calls for it, since organic residue passes ROSE.
Choosing the strongest solvent available as a default :: Match chemistry to plating and elastomers, because the cleaner can damage the assembly it saves.
Cleaning after floor life has expired without a bake :: Bake per IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033 first, because cleaning adds moisture rather than removing it.

## Glossary
ROSE :: Resistivity of solvent extract, a test of soluble ionic contamination expressed as sodium chloride equivalent.
Surface insulation resistance :: The resistance measured across a surface between conductors under humidity and applied bias.
Conductive anodic filament :: A copper filament that grows through laminate along glass fibres under bias and moisture.
Saponifier :: An alkaline additive that converts flux residue into a water-soluble soap for rinsing.
