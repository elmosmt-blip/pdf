## The bake decision starts with package and exposure
Bake or not is not a question about the part name. Take the moisture sensitivity level, the package type, whether the moisture barrier bag was opened, dry pack integrity, humidity indicator card status, desiccant condition and the planned time to reflow together.

A batch that will be consumed inside the remaining floor life in qualified dry storage may not need baking at all. Where exposure, cabinet performance or bag condition cannot be proven, treat the batch as exposed and bake or quarantine it. The person who controls the lot record makes the call; the line operator alone does not.

## Floor life by MSL
For plastic packages, IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033D assigns moisture sensitivity levels and floor life. Under typical ambient conditions MSL 3 has 168 h, MSL 4 has 72 h, MSL 5 has 48 h and MSL 5a has 24 h; higher levels follow supplier-specified conditions. Floor life begins when the dry pack opens or when the package meets ambient air.

Check the controlled revision and any supplier limit, because some suppliers shorten floor life for large packages, high moisture content or long reflow profiles.

## Dry storage suspends or extends exposure
IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033D recognises dry storage at 5 % RH or less as the condition that suspends the floor-life clock: below that humidity the device gains no moisture, so exposure stops accumulating. A common 10 % RH cabinet is not equivalent. It slows the clock instead of stopping it and leaves parts on a shortened budget, which makes 10 % RH a cheaper mitigation for devices with budget to spare and never a substitute for the 5 % RH requirement.

Calibrate, alarm and log the dry cabinet, and treat the qualified limit as the one written in your procedure. A cabinet out of range means the time inside it counts as floor life. Keep reels in sealed bags or dry cabinets after opening, and for trays and tubes use sealed containers with desiccant and a humidity indicator card when no cabinet is available.

## Bake conditions by package type
Read the bake table in IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033D for the package type, then the component or tape datasheet. Bake time in J-STD-033 Table 4-1 is indexed to package body thickness and to the moisture sensitivity level, with separate columns for floor life exceeded by more than 72 h and by 72 h or less, so all three variables come off the table before anyone quotes a time.

Tape and reel must not be baked above 40 °C, because carrier tape and cover tape soften and deform. The 40 °C column is therefore long: for bodies up to 1.4 mm it runs 8 days at MSL 2 up to 22 days at MSL 5a, and for 1.4 to 2.0 mm 25 to 79 days. A 48 h or 168 h bake at 40 °C will not dry a reel.

Loose parts in trays or tubes can use the 125 °C column, again by thickness and MSL: 5 to 16 h up to 1.4 mm, 18 to 48 h at 1.4 to 2.0 mm and 48 h at 2.0 to 4.5 mm, and only where the tray, tube and component materials are rated for it.

Table 4-2 is a different table. It covers the supplier dry-pack bake before packing at a fixed 24 h maximum exposure time, and its 125 °C figures — 7 to 28 h up to 1.4 mm and 18 to 48 h above that — must never be quoted as a user rebake recipe.

Above 90 °C the cumulative bake time is limited to 96 h unless the supplier states otherwise, because intermetallic growth and solderability loss accumulate. Check the tape, tray, tube and equipment datasheets: tape can soften, trays can warp and some coatings blister.

## PCBs and THT assemblies
Bare boards and THT components absorb moisture too, but the bake rule follows laminate, coating and package. Use the board supplier's bake condition; common practice is 105 °C to 125 °C for 2 h to 24 h, longer for thick boards or high moisture.

Apply the same MSL logic to THT plastic packages as to SMT. Bake before assembly when the board or component passed its qualified exposure or shows moisture indicators, and confirm that the bake temperature will not damage solder mask, silkscreen or plated features.

## Records that make the decision auditable
Record lot or reel ID, MSL, package type, dry pack open date and time, humidity indicator card reading, desiccant status, dry cabinet RH, bake start and end, bake temperature, controlled bake humidity, operator and disposition.

A batch released without bake records its remaining floor life and the planned reflow window. A baked batch records the recipe and any reflow qualification result. Keep the record with the lot so the next shift can see whether the floor life still holds.

## Bake or quarantine
Bake when the batch passed floor life, when the dry pack or desiccant failed, when the humidity indicator card is above its threshold, or when the dry cabinet ran out of range. Quarantine when the MSL is unknown, the package is damaged, the bake recipe is not qualified for the package, or the device has already been reflowed and the supplier requires requalification.

Never bake a package that cannot take the temperature or the time; use qualified lower-temperature dry storage or return the material to the supplier. The purpose is to stop moisture flashing in reflow, not to add a handling step.

## Key takeaways
Read bake time from J-STD-033 Table 4-1 by package thickness, MSL and how far floor life was exceeded, never from memory.
Respect the 40 °C ceiling for tape and reel, and expect days rather than hours at that temperature.
Suspend floor life only at 5 % RH or less; a 10 % RH cabinet slows the clock without earning any credit.

## Common mistakes
Applying a tray bake recipe to a tape-and-reel lot :: Keep reels at or below 40 °C, and use de-reeling or a qualified dry cabinet when the bake window is too long.
Quoting Table 4-2 as a user rebake recipe :: Table 4-2 is the supplier dry-pack bake before packing; take the user recipe from Table 4-1.
Treating a 12 % RH cabinet excursion as suspended floor life :: Count the excursion as exposure and bake or quarantine the lot.

## Glossary
Dry pack :: The moisture barrier bag, desiccant and humidity indicator card that protect a moisture-sensitive device before use.
Floor-life suspension :: The stop of floor-life accumulation that J-STD-033 grants at 5 % RH or less in qualified dry storage.
Cumulative bake time :: The total time a package has spent above 90 °C, limited because solderability degrades with it.
Humidity indicator card :: A card whose colour spots show the peak humidity inside a dry pack.
