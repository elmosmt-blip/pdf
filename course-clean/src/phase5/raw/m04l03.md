## Printer setup is a coupled process
Squeegee pressure, squeegee speed, snap-off, separation and wipe cycle are not independent knobs. Together they govern paste roll movement, aperture fill, paste release and residue management.

For a stated defect, change one variable at a time, inspect the deposit, and predict the direction of the next change from the paste and equipment datasheet.

## Squeegee pressure
Squeegee pressure is force per unit of blade length, never a bare force, because 100 N is a light touch on a 400 mm blade and a crushing load on a 100 mm blade. Low pressure leaves apertures underfilled, especially small ones or high aspect ratio stencils. High pressure drives paste under the stencil, wears the blade, deforms the stencil and disturbs the paste roll.

Industry guidance clusters around 0.45 to 0.70 kg per 25 mm of blade, which is 0.2 to 0.6 N/mm; work in N/mm and state the blade length with every setting, because 0.2 N/mm on a 250 mm blade is 50 N and 0.6 N/mm is 150 N. Start at the lower end and raise it until the stencil wipes clean, then verify against the paste and equipment datasheet and the printed deposit.

## Squeegee speed
Speed sets how long paste has to fill the apertures and how much paste roll is dragged ahead of the blade. Too slow and the roll sits longer, inviting bleed and smearing; too fast and fill time drops, air gets trapped and transfer falls short.

A common starting range is 20 to 80 mm/s, adjusted for fine pitch, paste viscosity and stencil thickness, and verified against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Snap-off
Snap-off is the controlled lift-off after the print stroke. It helps paste leave the aperture walls and keeps the stencil from dragging paste across the board.

A fast snap-off pulls paste out of the apertures or leaves tails; a slow one lets paste smear as the board or stencil moves. A common lift-off speed range is 5 to 30 mm/s, confirmed against the paste and equipment datasheet.

## Separation
Separation is the stencil-to-board gap at the start of the print stroke. Too small and the stencil contacts the board, bleeding paste and contaminating the roll; too large and transfer falls, the roll is pushed ahead and the stencil flexes more.

A common starting range is 0.5 to 1.5 mm, verified against your paste and equipment datasheet and the board support condition.

## Wipe cycle
The wipe manages residue on the stencil underside and the condition of the paste roll. Dry, wet and vacuum wipes behave differently. Too frequent wiping strips paste from apertures, disturbs the roll or pushes solvent into the paste; too infrequent wiping lets paste dry, clog apertures and reduce release.

A common dry wipe interval is every 1 to 5 prints, checked against your paste and equipment datasheet and solvent compatibility.

## Tuning method and direction of change
For insufficient paste, raise pressure within its band, slow the stroke if fill time is the limit, reduce separation if the gap is too large, and wipe more often if apertures are clogging.

For bleed or bridging, lower pressure, slow down if roll drag is suspected, increase separation if the gap is too small, and adjust snap-off for clean release. Confirm the result with deposit height and volume, 2D or 3D inspection, and the applicable IPC workmanship standard.

## Key takeaways
Express squeegee pressure per blade length in N/mm, because a bare force says nothing without the blade it acts on.
Change one setting at a time and predict the direction from the defect, so the next print tells you whether the model was right.
Verify every starting range against the paste and equipment datasheet before it becomes a production setting.

## Common mistakes
Raising squeegee pressure first for every low-volume defect :: Check fill time and separation first when pressure already sits mid-band.
Quoting pressure as a bare force :: Convert to N/mm and record the blade length, or the setting cannot be transferred to another machine.
Wiping more often to fix bleed :: Excess wiping strips paste and dries the stencil edge; fix the underside contamination instead.

## Glossary
Snap-off :: The controlled separation of the board from the stencil after the print stroke.
Separation :: The stencil-to-board gap set at the start of the print stroke.
Paste roll :: The bead of paste that rolls ahead of the squeegee and feeds the apertures.
Wipe cycle :: The interval and method used to clean the stencil underside between prints.
