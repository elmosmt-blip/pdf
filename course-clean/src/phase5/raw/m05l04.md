## Evidence sequence before naming a mechanism
Start from measured data, not from a visual guess. For each defect, collect SPI volume and pad location, pre-reflow AOI position and orientation, placement head logs, feeder slot and nozzle ID, the reflow profile, and post-reflow AOI or X-ray.

A component misplaced before reflow over centred paste of correct volume points to placement. Paste offset, volume error or a bridge before placement points to printing. Correct pre-reflow placement that shifts, lifts or rotates only after reflow points to wetting or the thermal profile.

Tombstone is usually a reflow wetting imbalance, though pre-reflow tilt or paste imbalance makes placement or printing contributory. Patterns by feeder, nozzle, board location and time separate systematic placement faults from random handling faults.

## Offset
Offset is the displacement of the component centre from the land centre. Read X and Y from AOI, then compute magnitude and direction.

Feeder pickup offset, tape pocket deformation, nozzle wear, vision calibration drift, board support sag and tape peel force all produce it. A consistent vector from one feeder slot points to feeder pickup or the tape pocket. A consistent direction by board location points to fiducial recognition, vision calibration or support. Random offset across feeders and nozzles points to vacuum, component drop or pocket release. Set action limits from your AOI limits, land geometry and equipment datasheet, because the acceptable offset depends on pad size, component tolerance and wetting.

## Skew
Skew is angular misalignment of the body against the land edges. Measure the angle from AOI and correlate it with nozzle ID, feeder slot and component orientation.

Rotation during pick, nozzle spin, a component sitting crooked in its pocket, tape pocket geometry, vision angle error and head rotation error all cause it. Skew with offset points to pick or pocket; skew without offset points to nozzle rotation or vision angle. Skew present in pre-reflow AOI belongs to placement; a component that rotates only in reflow belongs to wetting imbalance or the profile. Check angle limits against the AOI program and the equipment datasheet.

## Tilt and tombstone
Tilt is a non-coplanar body, and tombstone is severe tilt where one end lifts while the other stays wetted. Measure Z height, shadow, focus and, where needed, X-ray.

Nozzle height, component thickness variation, feeder pocket, board warp, paste height and reflow surface tension all play a part. Pre-reflow tilt points to placement, nozzle, feeder or board support; pre-reflow paste imbalance points to printing. Correct placement and paste followed by lift in reflow points to wetting imbalance, profile or board warp. Tombstone is often filed as a reflow defect, yet placement contributes whenever the part sits tilted, skewed or on uneven paste.

## Missing component
A missing part can come from an empty feeder, a failed pick, vacuum loss, a dropped component, a vision reject, a program skip or a pocket failure.

Diagnose in order: pick and place logs, feeder count, pre-reflow AOI, post-reflow AOI. No pick in the log points to an empty feeder, pocket or feeder count. A pick with no place points to vacuum, a drop or nozzle height. A logged place confirmed by pre-reflow AOI but absent after reflow points to reflow, transport or handling.

## Wrong part
Wrong part means the placed component does not match the BOM, the program or the expected value. Feeder misload, wrong reel, wrong program, manual substitution, a tape splice and feeder slot mismatch all produce it.

Compare the BOM with the feeder map, scan the reel barcode, review AOI component recognition, and use X-ray or electrical test where the marking is not visible. A wrong part in pre-reflow AOI belongs to feeder loading or the program; a correct part with a wrong value usually means a misload or a wrong reel. On THT, tray or tube loading and the insertion program enter the same way.

## Polarity
Polarity defects reverse a diode, IC, tantalum, electrolytic or another polarised part. Feeder orientation, pocket orientation, manual loading, a disabled vision polarity check, nozzle rotation and program angle all cause them.

Inspect the polarity mark or pin 1, review the pre-reflow AOI polarity check, and correlate with the feeder slot. A reversed part in pre-reflow AOI belongs to feeder orientation or the program; correct polarity that reverses later points to reflow rotation, handling or a manual step. On THT, insertion direction and tray orientation dominate.

## Attribution and corrective action
Attribution rests on the evidence sequence, not on one image: SPI, pre-reflow AOI, the reflow profile, post-reflow AOI or X-ray, and feeder and nozzle logs.

Systematic defects by feeder slot point to loading, pickup or pocket. Systematic defects by board location point to vision, fiducials or support. Random defects across feeders and nozzles point to vacuum, drop or pocket release. Defects that appear only after reflow point to wetting or the profile, and paste defects before placement point to printing.

Use your acceptance standard for disposition, but set action limits from process capability, land geometry and the equipment datasheet, and document containment, root cause, corrective action and verification data.

## Key takeaways
Capture SPI and pre-reflow AOI before reflow, because the position of the part before and after reflow is what separates placement from wetting.
Group defects by feeder slot, nozzle and board location, since a pattern names the mechanism faster than any single image.
Set placement action limits from pad geometry and component tolerance, not from a round number that ignores both.

## Common mistakes
Calling tombstone a reflow defect without checking pre-reflow tilt :: Read pre-reflow AOI first, because a tilted part tombstone even on a good profile.
Diagnosing a missing part from a post-reflow image alone :: Check the pick and place logs to see whether the machine ever picked it.
Treating a single offset sample as a direction :: Measure across boards, since scatter and a consistent vector need different fixes.

## Glossary
Skew :: Angular misalignment of a placed component against its land pattern.
Tombstone :: A chip component standing on one end after reflow because one joint wetted before the other.
Polarity defect :: A polarised component placed in the reverse orientation.
Action limit :: The measured value at which the process is stopped or investigated, set from capability and geometry.
