## Program scope and storage assumptions
An ESD control program is a management system, not a bag of gadgets. In storage the protected area is defined by the materials handled, the sensitivity of the parts, the packaging in use, and the people and equipment that move them. ANSI/ESD S20.20 and IEC 61340-5-1 both demand a documented program with written procedures, training, qualification and periodic audit.

Start the storage audit with the control plan, then check that the physical area matches it: EPA boundaries, entry controls, grounding provisions, packaging inventory, ionizer coverage where needed, and records. A missing or stale plan is the first gap, and it is programmatic rather than hardware.

## Grounding and equipotential bonding
Grounding in an EPA removes charge and holds conductive items at a common potential. The reference must trace to a building electrical ground or another qualified point, and the bonding network must be continuous and inspectable. ANSI/ESD S6.1 covers grounding and equipotential bonding; use it to confirm that racks, carts, shelving, tables and floor systems are bonded as the control plan requires.

Storage failures are usually the subtle ones: a cart or rack grounded at one point but isolated by casters, plastic feet, painted contact surfaces or a broken bonding jumper. Measure or inspect by the program method, and check the result against the equipment datasheet and site electrical safety requirements.

## Personnel grounding
Personnel grounding works through wrist straps, footwear and floor systems, or a combination, depending on the task and the control plan. ANSI/ESD S1.1 governs wrist straps, which suit static controlled workstations; footwear and floor systems are qualified under ANSI/ESD STM97.1 and ANSI/ESD STM97.2.

Wrist straps are impractical while moving pallets, but they still belong at receiving inspection, kitting and rework benches. Check that the chosen method suits the task, that the strap or footwear is worn correctly, that the floor is qualified for that footwear, and that the system is tested at the required interval. Where the plan allows an exception, document the risk basis and the compensating controls.

## Mats, surfaces and storage fixtures
Static dissipative or conductive mats cover benches and staging surfaces where people handle sensitive items. ANSI/ESD STM4.1 covers static dissipative worksurfaces, having superseded S4.1, and ANSI/ESD STM11.11 characterises surface resistance or resistivity.

In storage, mats get misused: laid on insulating shelves, grounded through a damaged cord, or treated as general work surfaces with no defined ground path. Verify that the mat connects to the EPA ground reference through the required resistor or bonding method, that the connection is intact, and that insulating packaging, tape or plastic trays do not bypass it. Check resistance limits against the mat datasheet and the site control plan.

## Ionization
Ionizers neutralise charge on isolated conductors and insulators that cannot be grounded. ANSI/ESD STM3.1 covers ionization, and performance qualification and periodic verification are judged against its offset-voltage and discharge-time limits; it is the ESDA counterpart of IEC 61340-4-7. Footwear and flooring, by contrast, are qualified under ANSI/ESD STM97.1 and STM97.2.

In storage, ionizers usually sit at unpacking, kitting or inspection stations where plastic bags, foam, labels and tape generate charge. Check placement, airflow, balance and maintenance: ionization does nothing if the ion stream is blocked, the emitter points are dirty, or the unit is out of balance. Verify balance and discharge time against the ionizer datasheet and the program acceptance criteria.

## Packaging and storage containers
Packaging is a primary storage control, because parts may sit in a container for hours or days. ANSI/ESD S541 covers ESD packaging, including shielding bags, conductive containers and closure methods.

Confirm that the packaging class matches part sensitivity and the storage environment, that shielding bags are closed and undamaged, and that containers suit the EPA grounding scheme. Storage frequently gets this wrong by putting a shielding bag on an insulating shelf and trusting it against every field. Shielding reduces field exposure; the package still has to be handled and stored as it was qualified. Check package type, closure and storage configuration against the packaging datasheet and the control plan.

## Audit method and risk ranking
A useful audit follows a checklist tied to the control plan and records evidence: photos, serial numbers, test dates, operator observations. Rank each gap by exposure, sensitivity and detectability.

High risk means many sensitive parts affected, repeated daily, undetected downstream. Medium risk is a local gap with limited exposure or partial detection. Low risk is documentation or housekeeping with little direct charge-transfer path. Deliver a corrective action list with owner, due date and verification method rather than a list of nonconformances.

## Key takeaways
Audit the storage EPA against its own control plan first, because a stale plan makes every downstream check meaningless.
Verify grounding by measurement to the reference, since a rack that looks bonded can be isolated by paint, casters or a broken jumper.
Rank gaps by exposure, sensitivity and detectability, and hand back actions with owners rather than a list of findings.

## Common mistakes
Treating a grounded-looking rack as bonded :: Measure the bonding path to the EPA reference and inspect contact surfaces for paint or insulation.
Using a 10 % RH cabinet or an unmonitored ionizer as a substitute for grounding :: Qualify each control to its own standard and keep the test records with the equipment.
Closing an audit finding on a visual check :: Verify closure with a repeat test or re-audit record.

## Glossary
ESD protected area :: A defined space in which all conductive items and personnel are bonded and sensitive items are handled under documented controls.
Equipotential bonding :: Connecting conductive items so they sit at the same potential and cannot discharge into each other.
Ionizer :: Equipment that neutralises static charge by delivering balanced positive and negative ions to the air.
Shielding bag :: Packaging that limits electrostatic field exposure to the parts inside it.
