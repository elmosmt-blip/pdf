## Containment and traceability are separate controls
Containment protects the customer while the cause is still unknown, and traceability tells you what to contain.

A defect can be created by paste, component, stencil, placement, reflow, wave solder, insertion, cleaning or a test escape. A good containment record starts at the detection point and works backward to the last verified good point.

It identifies the product, the process step, the equipment, the operator, the program revision, the material lots and the time window. Without that, containment is a guess with a quantity attached.

## Defining quarantine scope
Quarantine scope is a bounded suspect population.

Use timestamps from MES, ERP, test logs and line audits. The scope covers work in process, finished goods, shipped goods, returns, and anything made between the last verified good inspection and detection.

Include adjacent shifts and changeovers where the process was not verified afterwards. A paste lot change, stencil change, feeder change or wave program change usually widens the window, because the change itself is a candidate cause.

Scope too narrow lets the escape continue downstream, and the company may then have to notify the customer under the applicable requirement in ISO 9001 or IATF 16949.

## Lot trace in SMT and through-hole
Lot trace connects the assembly to the materials and process parameters that affected it.

On SMT, trace the component lot, solder paste lot, stencil, placement program, feeder setup, reflow profile and oven zone settings. On through-hole work, trace the component lot, insertion program, wave solder program, flux density, preheat temperature, conveyor speed and solder pot temperature.

Verify the settings against the equipment datasheets before quoting them as fact in the report.

## Retroactive search
Retroactive search looks for other units that may share the latent cause.

Search forward from the suspect materials and backward from the defect signature. Include units made before and after detection where the process was not verified, and units from the same equipment, operator, program or material lot.

Where the population is too large to inspect fully, use a sampling plan matched to the risk and state its confidence, because an unstated sample is an unstated claim. ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 can supply the plan when one is needed, and the plan is documented and approved rather than chosen at the bench.

## 8D structure for a line escape
An 8D report is a structured problem-solving record.

It names the team, describes the problem with product, quantity, location and detection point, and states the interim containment. It identifies the root cause and the escape point, then defines the permanent corrective action with implementation dates, owners and effectiveness checks.

Prevention comes next: update the control plan, the FMEA, the work instructions and the inspection program so the same escape cannot recur silently.

## Owners, dates and effectiveness metrics
Every containment and corrective action needs a named owner and a due date.

Containment actions are short-term and are verified before suspect material is released. Corrective actions get a longer validation window.

Measure effectiveness with a metric tied to the defect: solder defect ppm, first pass yield, rework rate, test escape rate or audit findings. Set the target from historical performance rather than from ambition, and re-measure after the fix is in place.

## Closing the loop
Closure needs evidence that the suspect population is dispositioned, the cause is corrected and the process is stable.

Dispositions are rework, scrap, use-as-is or customer concession, depending on contract and product requirements. Rework follows IPC-7711/7721 and is documented, and soldering acceptance is checked against IPC-A-610 and IPC/JEDEC J-STD-001 where applicable.

## Key takeaways
Bound the suspect population before releasing anything, because containment without a window is a promise rather than a control.
Trace the material and the process settings to each assembly, since a signature without a candidate cause leads nowhere.
Attach a metric and a date to every action, because an action without a measurement cannot be closed.

## Common mistakes
Setting the window at the detection time :: Start from the last verified good point, because defects created earlier were passing until now.
Quarantining without shipping data :: Include shipped and returned units, because the customer holds the rest of the population.
Closing an 8D on the corrective action alone :: Verify effectiveness after implementation, because a fix that was not measured is a hope.

## Glossary
Containment :: The immediate action that stops suspect product reaching the next process or the customer.
Traceability :: The record linking an assembly to its materials, equipment, programs and time window.
Retroactive search :: Identifying other units that share a suspect cause with the detected defect.
8D :: An eight-discipline problem-solving report structure used for escapes and recurring defects.
