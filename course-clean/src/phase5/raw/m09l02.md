## Scope and decision inputs
Flux selection for wave and selective wave starts from the condition of board and termination: base metal, finish, oxidation, hole geometry, component mass and acceptable residue.

On THT the flux must reach hole walls and lead surfaces. On a selective wave for SMT it must wet pad and termination without leaving excess residue under components. IPC J-STD-004 classifies flux by residue type and activity; confirm compatibility with the finish and the cleaning process on the datasheet.

## Solids and residue
Solids content drives residue, bridging and post-solder cleanliness. Low-solids no-clean flux leaves less visible residue and may need tighter preheat. Higher-solids rosin or water-soluble flux improves wetting on oxidised surfaces and carries more residue and cleaning.

Take the solids from the flux datasheet and check spray viscosity and residue acceptability against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Activity
Activity must be high enough to remove oxides through preheat and wave contact, and low enough to avoid corrosion, solder balling or residue that traps moisture.

The IPC J-STD-004 activity classes guide the choice: low activity for clean, fresh finishes, higher activity for oxidised surfaces or high-thermal-mass joints. Verify both by wetting and residue tests.

## Amount and pattern
Apply enough flux to wet every solderable surface and not enough to pool in holes, under components or on non-solderable areas.

With jetted or fogged flux, pattern uniformity matters more than peak thickness. Check coverage with flux indicator paper, residue inspection or cross-section. Nozzle pattern, pressure, board speed and solids set the amount; verify against your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Preheat
Preheat activates flux, drives off solvent and reduces thermal shock. The board temperature must be high enough to activate the flux before the wave and below the damage limits of board and components.

Measure with thermocouples on heavy pads and component bodies, and check the target against your paste, equipment datasheet and board material limits.

## Wave height and fog
Wave height sets the contact area and the turbulence. Too low gives incomplete hole fill; too high gives bridging, solder balls and pad erosion.

Set fog or selective wave height against board thickness and hole pattern, then verify by cross-section and visual inspection.

## Verification
Verification covers first-article wetting, hole fill, residue, cleanliness and reliability. IPC J-STD-001 and IPC-A-610 supply the acceptance criteria for soldered assemblies, used alongside your internal residue and cleaning limits.

## Key takeaways
Choose flux activity from the finish and the oxide on the board, then prove it by wetting and residue tests rather than by class alone.
Control flux solids rather than liquid volume, and state the basis whenever a deposition figure is quoted.
Set preheat from a board thermocouple, since flux activation happens at board temperature and not at the oven display.

## Common mistakes
Quoting flux deposition per unit of liquid when the specification is solids-based :: State the basis, because the two figures differ by several times.
Raising flux activity to fix a cold joint :: Check preheat and wave contact first, since activity cannot compensate for a cold board.
Judging flux coverage by the machine setting :: Measure with fluxometer or witness card, because pattern uniformity decides the result.

## Glossary
Solids content :: The non-volatile fraction of a flux that remains after the solvent evaporates.
Activity :: The ability of a flux to remove oxides from the metal surface during soldering.
Fluxometer :: An instrument that measures the flux solids deposited per unit area.
Fog height :: The height of the flux fog applied to the board underside before the wave.
