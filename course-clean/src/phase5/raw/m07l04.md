## Reading an X-ray image
A 2D X-ray image maps density: solder absorbs more than board laminate, and gas absorbs almost nothing. That is why a void appears dark inside a bright ball, and why a bridge appears as a bright path between two features that should be separate.

Read every image against a named criterion and a known feature size before calling anything. Magnification, detector resolution, focus and sample angle all change what the light and dark shapes appear to be.

Use 2D X-ray as the first method for hidden joints, and turn to laminar or 3D X-ray when overlapping features, multiple layers or true volumetric measurement are required. Boundary scan is not an imaging method: it tests selected digital interconnects electrically and finds opens or shorts only where the design supports it.

## BGA ball size and collapse
In a BGA image each ball should appear as a discrete, roughly circular feature of consistent diameter and spacing across the array. Ball size is read against the expected land pattern, paste volume, component specification and reflow profile.

A ball that is too small points to insufficient paste, paste bleed, poor stencil release or excessive reflow loss. One that is too large points to excessive paste, bleed between adjacent lands, or incomplete paste separation. IPC-7095 gives guidance on land pattern and paste printing design; verify the specific land size, aperture and volume against the component datasheet and your paste and equipment datasheet.

## Voiding in BGA balls
A void is a dark, low-density region inside a ball, because X-rays pass through gas more easily than through solder. Small voids are common and may be acceptable depending on size, location and product class.

IPC-A-610 carries the acceptability criteria for voids in solder balls, and for many Class 3 applications voids are commonly evaluated against a limit such as 25 % of the ball area, with the exact class, revision and customer requirement checked against IPC-A-610 and the contract. Voids at the outer edge of a ball, or voids coalescing across several balls, matter more than an isolated central void, because they cut the effective wetted area and raise thermal and mechanical risk.

## Bridging under BGA and fine-pitch parts
A bridge is a continuous high-density solder path joining two or more balls that should be isolated. On a BGA it often shows as a diagonal or horizontal line between adjacent balls near the array edge or under the die shadow.

Excessive paste volume, paste bleed, misalignment, collapsed balls and a ramp that lets solder flow laterally before wetting all cause it. IPC-7095 gives the land pattern and aperture design guidance; verify aperture size, pitch and stencil thickness against the paste and stencil datasheet. AOI may catch exposed bridges on fine-pitch QFN or QFP, while BGA bridges usually need X-ray.

## Through-hole fill and voids
Through-hole X-ray evaluates solder fill inside a plated hole, particularly when a component body, shielding or a second-side part hides the joint.

A sound through-hole joint shows solder wetting the pad, the hole wall and the lead, with a fill height suited to hole diameter and component class. IPC-A-610 carries the criteria for fill and voids, and for many Class 3 applications voids are commonly evaluated against a limit such as 25 % of the hole circumference, with class, revision and customer requirement confirmed against the standard and the contract. Verify hole diameter, lead diameter and required fill height against the board and component datasheets.

## Choosing the inspection method
Visual inspection covers exposed joints, polarity, missing components, tombstoning and obvious solder defects. AOI covers exposed SMT placement, fillet shape, tombstoning and many exposed bridges.

A 2D X-ray covers BGA voiding, ball size, hidden bridging and simple through-hole fill where overlapping layers do not obscure the feature. Laminar or 3D X-ray takes over with stacked BGAs, dense routing, many layers, or when void volume and depth must be separated from overlapping features. Boundary scan covers selected digital interconnects where the design supports it, detecting some opens and shorts without replacing X-ray for solder geometry.

## Disposition with a named criterion
Disposition rests on a named criterion, never on appearance. Accept when the measured defect sits inside the applicable IPC-A-610 class and the customer requirement.

Rework when the defect is repairable and the design, package and thermal budget allow repair without damaging nearby features; a localised BGA bridge may be reworkable if the land pattern and component survive the process. Reject when the defect breaks the named criterion and cannot be reworked reliably: a void over the allowed area, a bridge that cannot come out without lifting pads, or a through-hole joint that cannot reach the required fill.

Record the standard, the class, the measured value and the disposition every time.

## Key takeaways
Name the criterion and the class before judging an image, because the same void is acceptable in one class and a reject in another.
Compare measured feature sizes on the image itself, since magnification and angle change what the shapes appear to be.
Note the void location as well as its size, because an edge void threatens the joint more than a central one of equal area.

## Common mistakes
Calling a void from a single 2D projection :: Confirm size and depth with laminar or 3D X-ray when the joint is stacked or overlapped.
Applying a 25 % void limit without checking the class :: Read the applicability from IPC-A-610 and the contract before acceptance.
Deciding disposition from appearance rather than a measurement :: Record the measured value against the criterion so the call can be reproduced.

## Glossary
Void :: A gas-filled cavity inside a solder joint, visible on X-ray as a dark region.
Collapse :: The reduction in BGA ball height during reflow as the ball settles onto the land.
Die shadow :: The area under the die where the package mass changes heat flow and paste behaviour.
Laminar X-ray :: Tomographic X-ray that reconstructs a single slice so depth can be resolved.
